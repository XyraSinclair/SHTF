# Go-Bag

A 72-hour bag per person, pre-packed, by the door. Not aspirational camping gear — the things that get you through three days somewhere else.

## If you only read one line

**The best go-bag is the one you actually packed and know how to use.** A perfect list on a website is worth zero.

## The three-layer model

Think in three rings, not one bag:

1. **EDC (everyday carry)** — what's in your pockets right now. Phone, keys, wallet, small knife, lighter if you smoke or want to, whistle on keychain. 0 lb extra.
2. **Get-home bag** — kept in your car or desk. Enough to walk home in bad conditions. ~5–8 lb.
3. **Go-bag (72-hour)** — kept by the door. Evacuation scale. ~15–25 lb per adult.

Most people overbuild the go-bag and skip the first two. Don't.

## Packing list — 72-hour bag

Everything below assumes one adult. Scale down for kids (especially water/food, not documents).

### Water — the single most important item

- 1 × 1-liter reusable bottle (Nalgene, stainless, whatever)
- 1 × water filter (Sawyer Squeeze, MSR TrailShot, or similar, ~$25–45)
- 10 × water purification tablets (Aquatabs or Potable Aqua, ~$10 for pack)
- Optional: 1 × 2-liter collapsible bladder for carrying bulk water

Filter + tablets is redundant on purpose. Filters fail. Tablets have a shelf life. Together = resilient.

Citation: `survival-guides/Emergency_Water_Purification_Guide.pdf`.

### Food — three days, high calorie density

- 6–9 × high-calorie bars (Clif, Probar, or Millennium/Mayday bars — the latter last years)
- 2–3 × jerky or meat sticks
- 1 × peanut butter packet or small jar
- 6 × electrolyte tabs or packets (Nuun, LMNT, etc.)
- Spoon (metal, titanium if fancy, plastic is fine)

Do not pack heavy cookware or hope to cook. This is evacuation food, not camping food. Cold-eatable, long shelf life.

### Shelter — weather you will actually face

One season of clothing, not all four:

- 1 × weather-appropriate layer (rain shell in PNW, insulated jacket in winter)
- 1 × wool or synthetic base layer top
- 1 × pair merino or synthetic socks
- 1 × underwear change
- 1 × wool hat (warm seasons too — temperature drops at night)
- 1 × pair work gloves or cycling gloves
- 1 × emergency mylar blanket (SOL Emergency Blanket is better than the thin kind, ~$5)
- Optional: 1 × emergency bivy or tarp

Pack for the weather you are most likely to evacuate into. You are not climbing Denali.

### Light

- 1 × headlamp (Petzl Tikka or equivalent, ~$25). Hands-free matters.
- 1 × spare set of batteries (or a rechargeable with USB-C)
- 1 × small flashlight backup

Shake / hand-crank lights work but are slow. Headlamp + batteries is better.

### Fire

- 1 × Bic lighter (cheap, reliable, works wet with effort)
- 1 × ferrocerium rod + striker (backup for when the lighter fails)
- 6–10 × cotton balls in petroleum jelly in a small ziploc (best cheap tinder)
- Optional: waterproof matches

Practice lighting a fire with the ferro rod before you need to.

### First aid (compact)

- Nitrile gloves × 4
- Gauze pads × 6
- Roller gauze × 2
- Medical tape × 1 roll
- Tourniquet (CAT or SOF-T — real ones, not the stretchy bracelet knockoffs) × 1
- Hemostatic gauze (QuikClot Combat Gauze) × 1 — optional but cheap insurance
- Elastic bandage (ACE) × 1
- Triangle bandage × 1
- Small scissors (trauma shears better)
- Moleskin or Leukotape (blister prevention — this is what actually gets used)
- Ibuprofen (16 tablets)
- Acetaminophen (16 tablets)
- Antihistamine (16 tablets — Benadryl or similar)
- Anti-diarrheal (Loperamide / Imodium, 8 tablets)
- Electrolyte replacement (oral rehydration salts, 2 packets)
- Any personal meds (3-day supply, rotate every 3 months)
- Personal glasses / spare contacts if needed

Citation: `survival-guides/FM4-25.11_First_Aid_Manual.pdf` and [`../cards/first-aid.md`](../cards/first-aid.md).

### Tools

- 1 × multi-tool (Leatherman Wave, Victorinox Huntsman, or similar)
- 1 × fixed-blade or folding knife (secondary, in case the multi-tool is occupied)
- 1 × paracord (25–50 ft, 550 rated)
- 1 × roll duct tape (flatten around a pencil to save space)
- 1 × contractor trash bag × 2 (shelter, raincoat, water collection, cargo)
- 1 × safety pins × 6
- 1 × sewing kit (micro)
- Optional: 1 × folding saw (pocket chainsaw or Silky Pocketboy)

### Navigation

- 1 × paper map of your region (topo if possible)
- 1 × compass (baseplate, ~$20)
- 1 × pen + small waterproof notebook

Phone GPS is not navigation. When the battery dies, the paper map and compass are what you have.

Citation: `survival-guides/FM3-25.26_Map_Reading_Land_Navigation.pdf`.

### Communications

- 1 × phone with cable
- 1 × battery pack (10,000 mAh+, charged)
- 1 × wall charger
- 1 × car charger (if you have a car)
- Optional: 1 × Baofeng UV-5R with programmed frequencies + cable + manual

See `radio/UV-5R_Quick_Reference_Card.pdf` and [`../cards/radio-frequencies.md`](../cards/radio-frequencies.md).

### Documents and money

In a waterproof pouch or ziploc:

- Copies of IDs (passport, license)
- Copies of insurance cards
- Copies of critical prescriptions
- Emergency contact card (see [`01-household-roster.md`](01-household-roster.md))
- $100–200 cash, small bills
- List of account numbers (banking, insurance) — or just carry a small encrypted USB with photos of everything

### Sanitation and hygiene

- Toilet paper (compressed roll, ~$2)
- Unscented baby wipes (travel pack)
- Toothbrush + travel toothpaste
- Feminine hygiene products (if applicable, 3 days worth)
- Small hand sanitizer
- Optional: small pack of N95 respirators × 2 (fire smoke, dust, disease)

### Small extras that earn their weight

- Signal mirror (~$5)
- Whistle (~$3, attach to shoulder strap)
- 1 × bandana (towel, filter, wrap, sling, coffee filter)
- 1 × pair earplugs (sleeping near others is worse than you think)
- 1 × roll small cordage (bank line)
- 1 × photo of your family (sounds sentimental; it's actually practical — ID photo if you need to describe them to strangers)

## Kid-specific additions

For each child:

- Comfort item (plush, blanket, familiar item)
- 3 days of whatever they actually eat (shelf-stable version)
- 3 × snacks the kid recognizes
- Favorite small book or small toy
- ID card with parent names, phone numbers, address, medical info in the child's pocket
- Change of clothing appropriate to weather
- Any medications or inhalers in a labeled kit
- Diapers / wipes / formula for infants (extra: assume 4 days)

## Pet additions

- 3 days of kibble in a sealed bag
- 1 collapsible bowl
- Leash, harness
- Copies of vaccination / registration records
- Any pet medications
- Small blanket or towel with familiar scent

## Maintenance

Twice a year (daylight saving time is a good reminder):

- Check batteries. Replace any near end of life.
- Check water — replace if stored >6 months.
- Check food — replace anything expiring in the next 6 months.
- Check medications — rotate fresh.
- Re-check weather-appropriate layer (swap winter for summer if applicable).
- Re-read your [`01-household-roster.md`](01-household-roster.md) and update.

## What not to put in the go-bag

- Full-length firearm. Not practical, not legal in many evacuation contexts, and usually not a 72-hour priority.
- Large quantities of ammo. Weight.
- Entire first-aid trauma kit. If you need that level of care, you need a hospital, not more gear.
- Every tool you own.
- Books you haven't read.
- Precious metals.
- Everything your internet survival influencer recommended.

Weight discipline. Each extra pound makes the bag less likely to come with you.

## Before it happens, do this once

1. Buy or build the bag this weekend.
2. Pack it.
3. Walk a mile with it on. Adjust.
4. Put it by the door you would actually leave through.
5. Tell every household member where their bag is and what's in it.
