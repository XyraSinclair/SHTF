# The First Weekend

One weekend. Two to four hours of actual work. After this, you are more prepared than 95% of households.

## If you only read one line

**Do not buy gear first.** Do the cheap and free items on this list before ordering anything. Most of the value is in paperwork, decisions, and conversations — not equipment.

## The 2-hour version (if that's all you have)

1. Fill the bathtub or every clean container you have the next time a bad forecast hits. You just bought 40+ gallons of water for free.
2. Stock 14 days of calories per person from your normal grocery run — rice, beans, oats, peanut butter, canned fish, cooking oil, salt. $40–80/person. See [`03-water-food-stockpile.md`](03-water-food-stockpile.md).
3. Pick a designated shelter-in-place room in your home (interior, few windows, near plumbing).
4. Write your [`05-family-comms-plan.md`](05-family-comms-plan.md). One page. Takes 20 minutes.
5. Put $200 in cash somewhere in your home. Small bills.
6. Print [`../cards/`](../cards/) on your home printer. Put them in a labeled envelope.

That's it. You're done. Move on with your weekend.

## The full checklist

### Saturday morning: decisions and paperwork (free)

**Fill out the household roster.** [`01-household-roster.md`](01-household-roster.md) is a template. Names, DOBs, allergies, medications, insurance numbers, doctor contacts. One hour. **Keep it private — do not commit to git.**

**Write the family communications plan.** [`05-family-comms-plan.md`](05-family-comms-plan.md). Who calls whom. Where do you meet if you can't get home. Who is your out-of-state contact. 30 minutes, with everyone in the household present.

**Designate your shelter-in-place room.** Criteria: interior, fewest windows, access to water/bathroom, room for everyone to sleep on the floor. Usually a downstairs hallway or interior bathroom. 5 minutes.

**Identify two evacuation destinations.** Primary (friends/family within 50 miles) and backup (further away). Confirm with hosts. Drive the route to at least the primary. 1 hour including drive.

### Saturday afternoon: food and water (cheap)

**Water.** Buy or save enough to store 14 gallons per person (one gallon per person per day, 14 days). This is cheap and non-perishable.

Options:
- Case of 1-gallon jugs from the grocery store: ~$10 per person-week.
- Fill clean 2-liter soda bottles from the tap: free. Rotate every 6 months.
- 5-gallon water bricks: one-time ~$25 each, last years.

Store out of direct sunlight. Rotation: label dates, use oldest first.

**Food.** See [`03-water-food-stockpile.md`](03-water-food-stockpile.md). Key point: store what you actually eat. Rotate through it.

Minimum: 14 days of 2,000+ kcal/person/day. Built from:
- Rice (5 lb/person)
- Beans / lentils (3 lb)
- Oats (2 lb)
- Pasta (2 lb)
- Peanut butter (1 jar)
- Canned fish / meat (7 cans)
- Cooking oil (1 bottle)
- Salt, sugar, yeast (baseline amounts)
- Shelf-stable milk if you have kids (1–2 quarts)

Cost: $60–100 per person.

### Saturday evening: cash, docs, meds (cheap, high-ROI)

**Cash.** $200–500 in small bills. Stored in a discreet place inside the home. A second $200 in your car glovebox. A third $100 in your go-bag.

**Documents.** Take photos of: driver's license, passport, insurance cards, Social Security card, birth certificates, deeds/leases, prescription labels. Put them on an encrypted USB drive AND in encrypted cloud storage. See [`04-cash-and-documents.md`](04-cash-and-documents.md) for the full list.

**Medications.** Talk to your doctor about a 30-day extra supply. Most insurance will cover a one-time "vacation" refill. Controlled substances usually cannot be stockpiled — plan for continuity via your local pharmacy.

### Sunday morning: go-bag (one-time spend)

**Go-bag per person.** [`02-go-bag.md`](02-go-bag.md) has the packing list. Budget ~$50–150/person if you build from scratch; often much less if you already have camping gear.

Essentials:
- Water bottle + water filter (Sawyer Squeeze or similar, ~$25)
- High-calorie food, 3 days
- Weather-appropriate clothing layer
- Headlamp + spare batteries
- Small first aid kit
- Multi-tool or knife
- Cash
- Paper copies of critical docs
- Phone charger + battery pack
- Emergency blanket
- Whistle

Pack it. Put it by the door you would actually leave through. Do not store it in the attic.

### Sunday afternoon: digital and comms (free to cheap)

**Digital hardening.** [`06-digital-hardening.md`](06-digital-hardening.md). The single highest-leverage item: set up a password manager and turn on 2FA everywhere. Takes 2 hours the first time; saves you from most cyber-incident personal impact.

**Radio.** Cheap option: a $30 hand-crank AM/FM/NOAA radio from any hardware store. Enough to receive emergency broadcasts.

Better option: a Baofeng UV-5R (~$25). Program it with local repeaters, NOAA weather channels, and emergency frequencies. See `radio/UV-5R_Programming_Cheat_Sheet.pdf` and `radio/Baofeng_UV-5R_Programming_Guide.pdf`. Legal to own; transmitting on ham frequencies requires a Technician license (one test, ~$15).

**Print the cards.** [`../cards/`](../cards/) are designed to print one page each. Run them through a home printer. Put them in the car, the go-bag, and a kitchen drawer.

### Sunday evening: test and talk

**Walk the plan.** Out loud, with everyone in the household. "If the power goes out tonight, what do we do?" "If we have to leave in 20 minutes, what are we taking?" "If phones don't work, how do we meet?"

Find the holes now, not later.

**Tell one person outside the household.** Your out-of-area contact from the comms plan. They need to know they're on it.

## What to skip

Don't spend Sunday afternoon shopping for:

- Mylar survival tents (your existing blanket is fine)
- Freeze-dried meal kits (normal food is cheaper and you'll actually eat it)
- MOLLE tactical backpacks (a regular backpack is fine)
- Night-vision anything
- A second, third, or fourth firearm
- Precious metals (cash is more useful in an actual short-to-medium crisis)
- A bunker subscription service

These purchases feel productive and accomplish nothing useful.

## What to do on week two

You are allowed to breathe. Over the next few weeks, at your pace:

- Take a Stop the Bleed class (free, ~90 minutes, often at hospitals and fire stations).
- Take a CPR / first aid class.
- Meet your neighbors. Know who has mobility issues, medical needs, or kids. You are each other's first responders.
- Read [`../scenarios/`](../scenarios/) at dinner — one scenario per week is fine.
- Practice: run a water filter, light a fire, read a paper map, program the radio.

## Cost summary

| Item | Typical cost for a family of 4 |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 14-day food store | $240–400 |
| 14-day water store | $40–200 |
| Go-bags (×4) | $200–600 |
| Cash reserve | $500–1,000 (your own money, just set aside) |
| Encrypted USB for docs | $15 |
| Hand-crank radio | $30 |
| Baofeng + programming cable | $35 |
| Password manager subscription | $0–60/yr |
| **Total out-of-pocket** | **~$560–1,340** |

Everything else on this list is free. The paperwork and conversations matter more than the gear.

## Before anything happens, do this once

That's this whole document. Start. The prep you do now is the only prep you'll ever actually have when you need it.
