# Post-Disaster Recovery
The first 72 hours are about staying alive. The next year is about rebuilding a life. This is where the second disaster — financial, bureaucratic, emotional — hits.

## If you only read one line
Start a recovery binder today, log every call, keep every receipt, and treat recovery like a job with office hours.
Use this folder after immediate life safety.
It covers insurance, FEMA, housing, records, contractors, money, mental health, and household strain.
The goal is to avoid avoidable ruin while you rebuild.
Use it when:
- the immediate danger has passed
- the household still has no stable home, money path, or records path
- decisions now will affect the next year

## The Recovery Binder
Use one physical 3-ring binder.
Mirror it in one cloud folder.
Name the cloud folder:

```text
DISASTER-RECOVERY-YYYY-MM-DD
```
The binder holds claim numbers, FEMA and SBA numbers, policy declarations, adjuster contacts, housing receipts, repair estimates, contractor proof, photos, inventories, medical receipts, mileage logs, school letters, mail-forwarding proof, appeal letters, denial letters, and every payment notice.
Use tabs: Insurance, FEMA, Housing, Receipts, Contractors, Money, Documents, Health, School, Legal, Diary.
Keep the binder boring.
Do not make it pretty.
Make it complete.

## The three habits that make recovery survivable

1. Log every call in a dated claims diary.
   Write date, time, person, phone number, claim number, promise made, and next step.

2. Keep every receipt.
   Keep paper copies in the binder. Photograph them the same day. Upload the photo.

3. Take 15 minutes a day of deliberate non-disaster time.
   Walk. Sit outside. Read. Pray. Call a friend and do not talk about forms.

## 5 most important actions

1. Start the binder before the first claim call.
2. Photograph damage before cleanup changes the scene.
3. Register for FEMA only after confirming Individual Assistance is declared.
4. Ask every creditor for disaster hardship treatment before you miss a payment.
5. Schedule daily non-disaster time and protect it like medication.

## Common mistakes

- Throwing away damaged items before insurance or FEMA sees them.
- Trusting a verbal promise from an adjuster, contractor, landlord, or creditor.
- Mixing insurance money with normal spending money.
- Missing mail after displacement and finding out through a late fee.
- Letting one exhausted person run the whole recovery for months.
- Treating emotional collapse at month three as weakness instead of a known phase.

## Before it happens, do this once
Make the empty binder now.
Add copies of your insurance declarations, IDs, titles, deeds, prescriptions, school contacts, pet records, and one page listing every account you would need after a loss.
