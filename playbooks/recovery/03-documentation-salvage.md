# Documentation Salvage

## If you only read one line
Save life-safety documents first, freeze wet records you cannot dry within 48 hours, and scan everything before the next move.
Paper loss becomes bureaucratic loss.
Bureaucratic loss becomes money loss.
Do not try to save every scrap equally.
Sort by what unlocks food, shelter, money, care, and legal identity.

## Priority pyramid

### Tier 1: identity, property, and irreplaceable proof
Save these first if it is safe:
- driver's license or state ID
- passports
- birth certificates
- marriage certificates
- death certificates
- Social Security cards
- immigration documents
- military DD-214
- wills
- trusts
- powers of attorney
- deeds
- vehicle titles
- insurance declarations
- prescription lists
- medical device records
- custody orders
- adoption records
- recent photos of household members
- pet vaccination records
Tier 1 documents unlock replacement of other documents.
They also prove ownership, identity, and family authority.

### Tier 2: financial and claim proof
Save these next:
- tax returns for the last 7 years
- W-2s and 1099s
- mortgage statements
- lease
- bank statements
- credit card statements
- loan statements
- property tax bills
- appraisals
- receipts for high-value property
- home inventory
- contractor records
- vehicle records
- business insurance policies
- business licenses
Seven years is a practical tax-record target.
Keep longer if business, property basis, inheritance, or open disputes are involved.

### Tier 3: business, school, and personal archives
Save after Tier 1 and Tier 2:
- business customer records
- invoices
- payroll records
- school records
- diplomas
- professional licenses
- family letters
- albums
- journals
- children's art
- genealogy
Some Tier 3 items are emotionally priceless.
Do not let that erase the order.
Get identity and claim documents secure first.

## Safety first
Do not enter an unsafe structure to save paper.
Do not handle sewage-soaked records barehanded.
Do not bring mold into a sleeping area.
For contaminated papers, wear:
- N95 or better respirator
- nitrile gloves
- eye protection if splashing is possible
- long sleeves
- washable shoes or boots
Wash hands after handling.
Keep salvaged wet records away from food areas.
If papers are soaked with sewage, fuel, chemicals, or fire-suppression runoff, health comes first.
Photograph them.
Bag them.
Save only what is worth the contamination risk.

## First sorting pass
Make four piles:
- dry and safe
- damp
- wet
- contaminated
Do not stack wet paper higher than needed.
Wet stacks fuse, tear, and mold.
Label boxes before moving:

```text
Owner:
Room found:
Contents:
Condition:
Date packed:
Photos taken:
```
Save labels from damaged folders and boxes.
They preserve context.

## Wet-paper salvage
Work in a cool, dry place.
Use clean tables, towels, or screens.
Air movement is good.
Direct heat is bad.
Direct fans on brittle originals are bad.
Do:
- separate pages gently
- support each page with both hands
- interleave absorbent paper between pages
- change blotting paper often
- dry flat when possible
- keep original order with small labeled slips
- photograph pages before separating if they may tear
Do not:
- pull stuck pages apart by force
- use an oven
- use a hair dryer
- place documents in direct sun
- scrub mud off ink
- use bleach
- laminate wet or damaged originals
If pages are stuck, stop.
Freeze the stack and deal with it later.

## Freezing for preservation
Freezing buys time.
It slows mold and prevents more damage while you arrange drying.
Use freezing when:
- there are too many papers to dry within 48 hours
- coated or glossy paper is wet
- photos are wet and cannot be dried right away
- records are important but you are exhausted
- the house is humid and drying will fail
Pack:
- plastic freezer bags
- small flat bundles
- labels inside and outside
- wax paper or freezer paper between groups if available
Seal bags.
Freeze flat.
Do not overpack one giant bag.
Small batches thaw and dry better.
A frost-free freezer may slowly dry items over time, but do not count on it for important records.
For major losses, a document-drying vendor can vacuum freeze-dry records.
That vendor's job is to stabilize wet paper at scale, dry it with controlled methods, and preserve order.
Use one for legal, business, archival, or high-volume records when the cost is justified.

## Photo salvage
Photos are fragile when wet.
The image layer can slide, scratch, or stick.
Handle by edges.
Keep the emulsion side up.
Do not rub the image surface.
Do not stack wet photos face to face.
Do not peel photos from albums if they resist.
For loose wet prints:
- rinse gently in clean cool water only if muddy and not fragile
- lay image side up on clean absorbent material
- keep air moving in the room
- change the absorbent material as it wets
- scan or photograph when dry enough to handle
For albums:
- photograph each page before touching it
- interleave absorbent paper where possible
- freeze if pages are sticking or drying will take too long
For framed photos:
- remove from frame if safe
- keep any labels
- dry photo and backing separately
- do not force stuck glass away from the print

## Digital salvage
Water plus electronics means stop.
Pull power.
Do not turn the device on "just to check."
Do not charge it.
Do not plug in a wet drive.
Do not use rice as a serious data-recovery plan.
For phones and laptops:
- power off if still on
- remove from case
- remove battery only if designed for user removal
- blot exterior
- keep the device cool and dry
- photograph serial numbers
- contact the maker or a data-recovery firm if data matters
For external drives:
- label the drive
- note whether it was powered during water exposure
- keep it dry after removal
- do not open it yourself
- do not run disk utilities
HDDs and SSDs fail differently.
Hard drives have platters and moving heads.
A recovery lab may clean, repair, and clone the media.
SSDs have flash chips and controller logic.
Encryption, controller failure, and TRIM can make recovery harder.
A data-recovery pro does not "fix your computer."
They stabilize the media, clone what can be read, and extract files from the copy.
Use one when the lost data is worth the bill.

## Cloud mirror
Every salvaged document gets two lives:
- physical binder
- cloud folder
Scan or photograph:
- front
- back
- envelope
- stamp or seal
- signature page
- notary page
- attached receipt
Use clear filenames:

```text
YYYY-MM-DD_document-type_person_or_property.pdf
```
Examples:

```text
2026-04-19_homeowners-policy_declarations.pdf
2026-04-19_birth-certificate_jane-doe.pdf
2026-04-19_fema-letter_application-123456789.pdf
```
Upload to two services if you can.
One can be your normal cloud drive.
The second can be a shared family folder, encrypted backup, or trusted out-of-area contact.
Do not send identity documents through random message threads unless there is no better option.

## The Recovery Binder
The binder is not a scrapbook.
It is an evidence machine.
Use:
- 3-ring binder
- tab dividers
- sheet protectors
- zip pouch for receipts
- notebook paper for call logs
- permanent marker
- USB drive if you maintain one
Create these tabs:
- IDs
- Insurance
- FEMA
- SBA
- Housing
- Receipts
- Medical
- School
- Contractors
- Taxes
- Legal
- Photos index
- Call log
Print key cloud-folder index pages once a week.
If the phone dies, the binder still works.

## 5 most important actions

1. Sort documents by Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 before drying work starts.
2. Wear N95 and nitrile gloves for moldy or sewage-contaminated papers.
3. Air-dry small batches flat in a cool dry place without heat.
4. Freeze wet records you cannot dry within 48 hours.
5. Scan or photograph every salvaged document into two digital locations.

## Common mistakes

- Spending hours on sentimental papers while IDs and insurance documents rot.
- Pulling stuck wet pages apart and destroying the only original.
- Using heat, sun, or a hair dryer on fragile records.
- Turning on a wet laptop or drive to see if it still works.
- Keeping moldy papers in a bedroom or kitchen.
- Scanning only the front page and missing seals, backs, and attachments.

## Before it happens, do this once
Scan Tier 1 and Tier 2 documents now, store copies in the recovery binder and cloud folder, and give one sealed copy to a trusted out-of-area person.
