# Financial Recovery After a Disaster

## If you only read one line
Call creditors before you miss payments, separate disaster money from normal money, and apply for FEMA, SBA, unemployment, and tax relief on time.
The money phase is slow.
Bills are not.
Assume every creditor needs notice.
Assume every aid program needs proof.
Assume every dollar must be traceable.

## Triage the creditors
Make a list in this order:
- mortgage or rent
- utilities
- auto loan
- insurance premiums
- medical debt
- credit cards
- personal loans
- student loans
- subscriptions
- taxes
Call before the due date.
Use the word "disaster."
Say:
- "My home or income was affected by a declared disaster."
- "I need disaster hardship options."
- "What forbearance, payment deferral, fee waiver, or credit-reporting protection is available?"
- "Will interest continue?"
- "Will missed payments be reported?"
- "Will the loan mature with a balloon payment?"
- "Send the terms in writing."
Log the call.
Do not accept "skip a payment" without asking what happens after the skipped payment.

### Mortgage
If your mortgage is federally backed or owned by Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, FHA, VA, or USDA, formal disaster-relief options may apply.
Ask the servicer:
- who owns or guarantees the loan
- whether a disaster forbearance is available
- how long it lasts
- how repayment works
- whether foreclosure activity is paused
- whether late fees are waived
- whether credit reporting is protected
- whether property-inspection or insurance-claim rules apply
Do not stop paying without written terms.
Forbearance pauses or reduces payment.
It usually does not erase the debt.

### Rent
Call the landlord early.
Ask for:
- temporary rent plan
- fee waiver
- lease break terms if the unit is uninhabitable
- written habitability inspection status
- deposit handling
- forwarding address process
If the rental is damaged, photograph conditions before leaving.
Keep all texts and notices.
Local tenant law matters.
A legal-aid housing lawyer helps by reading the lease, applying state habitability law, stopping illegal lockouts, and forcing written repair or lease-break terms.

### Auto loan
Call the lender if the vehicle is damaged or income is interrupted.
Ask:
- deferment
- fee waiver
- credit-reporting treatment
- gap coverage if totaled
- insurance claim instructions
- title-release process after payoff
Do not abandon a damaged financed vehicle without notifying the lender and insurer.

### Credit cards and personal loans
Ask for:
- disaster hardship plan
- lower minimum payment
- skipped-payment option
- late-fee waiver
- interest-rate reduction
- credit-reporting protection
- statement due-date change
Get the plan in writing.
Keep paying a token amount only if the creditor confirms it helps.

## Disaster Unemployment Assistance
Disaster Unemployment Assistance, or DUA, can cover people who are unemployed as a direct result of a major disaster and are not eligible for regular unemployment insurance.
It may cover:
- self-employed workers
- farmers
- gig workers
- workers who cannot reach the workplace
- workers whose workplace closed because of the disaster
- people scheduled to start work that no longer exists because of the disaster
- household breadwinners who became breadwinner because the head of household died in the disaster
DUA depends on a Presidential major disaster declaration that authorizes it.
Apply through your state unemployment agency.
File fast.
State disaster notices set the filing deadline.
The normal unemployment waiting week may be waived or handled differently under the disaster notice.
Do not wait to confirm every document.
File, then supplement.
Keep:
- proof of identity
- proof of employment or self-employment
- tax return
- 1099s
- invoices
- business license
- client letters
- photos of damaged workplace
- road closure or evacuation orders
- employer closure notice

## Tax implications
Federally declared disasters can trigger special tax rules.
The key phrase is "federally declared disaster."
Casualty losses for personal-use property are generally limited to federally declared disasters under current federal law.
Insurance reimbursement reduces the loss.
FEMA grants and insurance payments may have different tax treatment depending on what they pay for.
Do not guess.
Track:
- pre-disaster basis if known
- fair market value before loss if needed
- fair market value after loss if needed
- insurance payments
- FEMA payments
- SBA loan proceeds
- repair receipts
- debris removal
- appraisal costs
- photos
- inventories
IRS Section 165 can allow a disaster loss deduction.
IRS rules may allow you to elect to claim a disaster loss on the prior-year return for a faster refund.
That can matter when cash is gone.
Ask a tax preparer or CPA to do specific work:
- determine whether your disaster qualifies
- calculate casualty loss after insurance
- decide current-year versus prior-year claim
- prepare Form 4684
- amend a prior return if useful
- document basis for real property
- separate personal, rental, and business losses
This is worth paid help when the loss is large, property basis is unclear, or business assets are involved.

## SBA disaster loans
SBA disaster loans are not only for businesses.
SBA can offer disaster loans to:
- homeowners
- renters
- businesses
- private nonprofit organizations
Homeowners may apply for physical damage to a primary residence.
Renters and homeowners may apply for personal property losses.
Businesses may apply for physical damage and economic injury when authorized.
Terms can run up to 30 years.
Rates and limits depend on the program and whether credit is available elsewhere.
Apply even if you think you may not qualify when FEMA refers you.
In some cases, FEMA uses the SBA decision to decide eligibility for certain Other Needs Assistance.
A loan is not a grant.
Do not borrow simply because the money is offered.
Use SBA loan money only for eligible disaster recovery costs.
An SBA loan officer helps by explaining eligible uses, collateral, repayment terms, disbursement steps, and document requests.
They do not decide whether the debt is wise for your household.
Run the payment through your post-disaster budget before accepting.

## Separate bank account
Open a separate account for disaster money if you can.
Deposit:
- insurance checks
- FEMA payments
- SBA loan disbursements
- disaster charity grants
- employer disaster aid
- tax refunds tied to the loss
Pay recovery expenses from that account.
This creates a clean trail.
It prevents grocery money, claim money, and rent money from becoming one blur.
Label transfers:

```text
ALE hotel week 3
roof tarp reimbursement
FEMA rental assistance
SBA repair draw
```
Keep receipts in the binder and the cloud folder.

## Retirement withdrawal
Do not touch 401(k), IRA, or pension money first.
Retirement withdrawals can create:
- income tax
- 10% early-distribution penalty if no exception applies
- lost employer match
- lost market recovery
- loan repayment problems if job changes
- reduced bankruptcy protection depending on account and law
Disaster-specific tax relief sometimes creates special withdrawal or loan rules.
Those rules are narrow and time-bound.
Check the IRS disaster page for your event before acting.
A tax preparer helps by comparing:
- hardship withdrawal
- retirement-plan loan
- home-equity option
- SBA loan
- insurance advance
- family loan
- credit-card hardship plan
- amended tax return refund
Pick the least damaging money first.

## Post-disaster budget rebuild
Build a 90-day disaster budget.
Use current reality, not pre-disaster habits.
Categories:
- temporary housing
- normal housing payment if still due
- utilities at old and temporary address
- food
- restaurant overage
- fuel and mileage
- laundry
- storage
- insurance deductibles
- medical
- child care
- pet boarding
- school transport
- replacement clothing
- document fees
- contractor deposits
- minimum debt payments
Mark each category:
- covered by insurance
- covered by FEMA
- possible SBA
- out of pocket
- unknown
Then decide:
- what gets paid now
- what gets hardship call
- what waits
- what needs an appeal
- what needs family or charity help

## Predatory loan red flags
Disaster lenders know people are desperate.
Red flags:
- storefront "home repair loan" with high fees
- contractor financing that hides the rate
- prepaid fee for FEMA or SBA help
- fake FEMA representative asking for money
- "guaranteed grant" for a fee
- pressure to borrow before insurance pays
- title loan using your only vehicle
- payday loan rollover
- loan documents without total repayment amount
- blank spaces
- no right to review before signing
FEMA never charges an application fee.
SBA does not use random fee collectors at your door.
Report suspected fraud to FEMA, SBA, the FTC, your state attorney general, or local law enforcement.

## 5 most important actions

1. Call mortgage, rent, auto, and credit-card creditors before the first missed payment.
2. Apply for DUA quickly if work or self-employment was lost because of the disaster.
3. Track insurance, FEMA, SBA, and tax-related money in a separate account.
4. Apply for SBA if FEMA refers you, even if you expect denial.
5. Check IRS disaster relief before using retirement funds or filing the casualty loss.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until accounts are delinquent before asking for disaster hardship.
- Treating forbearance as forgiveness and ignoring the repayment terms.
- Mixing disaster funds with normal spending until proof is impossible.
- Skipping the SBA step and blocking possible FEMA help.
- Raiding retirement accounts before checking insurance advances, SBA, tax refund, or hardship plans.
- Taking a high-rate repair loan because the contractor says insurance will pay later.

## Before it happens, do this once
Write down every creditor, account number, login, hardship phone number, and insurance deductible in the recovery binder.
