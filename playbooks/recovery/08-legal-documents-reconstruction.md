# Legal Documents Reconstruction

## If you only read one line
Replace state ID first, then vital records, Social Security, passport, property records, court records, and immigration documents in that order.
Lost documents slow everything.
Do not replace them randomly.
Build the identity chain.
Each document unlocks the next.

## Order of operations
Use this order unless a specific emergency changes it:

1. Driver's license or state ID
2. Birth certificate
3. Social Security card or SSA proof
4. Marriage, divorce, custody, adoption, or death records
5. Passport
6. Immigration documents
7. Deeds, titles, and property records
8. Wills, trusts, powers of attorney
9. Military records
10. Professional, school, and business records
If immigration status, custody, medical authority, or military benefits are at risk, move that document higher.
Do not wait for the whole list to be perfect.
Start the first replacement today.

## Driver's license or state ID
The DMV or state licensing agency is first because photo ID unlocks banks, records, pharmacies, flights, hotels, and government offices.
Check your state DMV site.
Search terms:

```text
replace driver license disaster fee waiver [state]
replace state ID disaster [state]
```
Bring or upload what the state accepts:
- proof of identity
- Social Security number or proof of ineligibility
- proof of residence
- lawful status documents if required
- name-change documents
- police or disaster report if requested
If you are displaced, ask:
- Can temporary shelter address be used?
- Can mail-forwarding proof be used?
- Is there a disaster fee waiver?
- Is a temporary paper ID available?
- Can REAL ID requirements wait?
Keep the receipt.
Some agencies can verify your prior license record and issue a duplicate faster than a first-time ID.
Ask for duplicate replacement, not a new first issuance, if you had an existing license.

## Birth, marriage, and death certificates
Start with the state or county Vital Records office.
Use official government pages.
Do not start from a search ad.
VitalChek is the authorized third-party order service used by many state and county vital-records agencies.
Safest path:
- go to the state or county Vital Records page
- follow its link to VitalChek if listed
- avoid aggregator sites that add fees and delays
You may need:
- full name on record
- date of birth, marriage, divorce, or death
- place of event
- parents' names for birth records
- spouse name for marriage records
- proof of relationship
- photo ID
- mailing address
- payment
Ask about:
- disaster fee waiver
- expedited certified copy
- local pickup
- alternate ID if your ID was destroyed
- records held by county versus state
Order certified copies.
Plain photocopies often do not work for banks, passports, courts, or benefits.

## Social Security card
Replacing a Social Security card is free.
Use SSA.gov or a Social Security office.
Phone:

```text
1-800-772-1213
```
Online replacement may be available through a my Social Security account if your state and situation qualify.
SSA limits replacement cards:
- 3 per year
- 10 per lifetime
Legal name changes, citizenship changes, and some other exceptions may not count the same way.
Do not pay a private site to replace a Social Security card.
You may need proof of:
- identity
- age
- citizenship or lawful status
If you need the number for work, banking, or aid, an old W-2, tax return, SSA benefit letter, or pay stub may help until the card arrives.
Protect the number.
Do not email it unless the agency provides a secure method.

## Passport
Use:

```text
https://travel.state.gov
```
If the passport was lost or destroyed, report it.
Form DS-64 reports a lost or stolen passport.
A replacement generally requires a new passport application process.
You may need:
- proof of citizenship
- photo ID
- passport photo
- fees
- DS-64 if lost or stolen
- travel proof if requesting urgent service
For life-or-death emergency travel, use State Department emergency passport instructions and be ready to prove the emergency and travel date.
Do not use unofficial passport expediters unless you understand the fee and they are doing something you cannot do yourself.
Most expediters cannot override State Department rules.

## Deeds and property records
Recorded deeds are usually held by the county recorder, register of deeds, clerk, or land records office.
Search:

```text
[county] recorder deed copy
[county] register of deeds certified copy
```
Ask for:
- certified copy of deed
- mortgage or deed of trust records
- lien records
- property tax statement
- subdivision or plat record if needed
Fees are set by the county.
Ask if disaster fee waivers apply.
You usually do not need the original deed to prove ownership if the deed is recorded.
You need a certified copy or official record.
Vehicle titles come from the state motor vehicle agency.
Boat titles may come from motor vehicles, fish and wildlife, or natural resources depending on state.
Manufactured home titles may be motor vehicle titles, housing agency records, or county property records depending on state.

## Wills, trusts, and powers of attorney
Call the attorney who drafted the estate documents.
Ask:
- Do you hold the original will?
- Do you hold signed trust documents?
- Do you hold copies only?
- Who is named executor, trustee, agent, or successor?
- Are witnesses and notary details available?
- Should new originals be signed because the old originals were destroyed?
Original wills matter.
A copy may create probate problems depending on state law.
An estate attorney helps by:
- checking whether a copy can be used
- preparing new originals
- supervising required witnesses and notary
- updating agents, trustees, and guardians
- making sure financial institutions will accept the power of attorney
If the disaster changed property, family, guardianship, or health status, review the documents instead of only replacing them.

## Military records
DD-214 and military service records come from the National Archives for many veterans.
Use:

```text
https://www.archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records
```
You may need:
- full name used during service
- Social Security number or service number
- branch
- dates of service
- date and place of birth
- next-of-kin proof for some requests
DD-214 affects:
- VA benefits
- burial benefits
- employment preference
- loan programs
- state benefits
If benefits are at risk, request early.

## Marriage, divorce, adoption, and custody decrees
Marriage certificates usually come from Vital Records or county clerk.
Divorce decrees usually come from the court that issued the divorce.
Adoption records may be sealed and may require court process.
Custody orders come from the court of jurisdiction.
Search:

```text
[county] court records divorce decree copy
[county] family court custody order certified copy
```
Ask for certified copies.
If a custody exchange, school enrollment, medical consent, or travel issue is active, tell the clerk the document is urgent.
A family-law attorney helps by:
- getting emergency certified orders
- filing replacement or enforcement motions
- clarifying who has authority for school, medical, travel, and relocation decisions
- stopping informal post-disaster custody changes from becoming a fight later

## Green cards and immigration documents
Immigration documents are high-stakes.
Do not delay.
For a lost, stolen, or destroyed Permanent Resident Card, use USCIS Form I-90.
Use:

```text
https://www.uscis.gov/i-90
```
Other documents may require different forms:
- Employment Authorization Document
- naturalization certificate
- refugee travel document
- I-94
- visa in passport
- approval notices
Check USCIS for the exact document.
If deadlines, status, travel, employment, or removal risk is involved, use an immigration legal-aid provider or immigration attorney.
They help by:
- choosing the correct form
- preserving status proof
- requesting fee waiver if eligible
- replacing work authorization
- handling urgent travel or court issues
- avoiding filings that create risk
Do not rely on a notario.
In the United States, a notario is not the same as an attorney.

## Business and professional records
Replace:
- professional licenses
- business license
- EIN letter if available
- sales tax permit
- payroll records
- insurance certificates
- client contracts
- corporate records
- operating agreement
- board minutes
- tax records
Use:
- state licensing board
- secretary of state
- IRS business account records
- payroll provider
- insurer
- accountant
- bank
If business interruption insurance, SBA, taxes, or payroll are active, reconstruct business records before optional personal archives.

## 5 most important actions

1. Replace driver's license or state ID first because it unlocks most other requests.
2. Order certified vital records from state or county agencies, not search-ad sites.
3. Replace the Social Security card through SSA only and use 1-800-772-1213 for help.
4. Report and replace lost passports through travel.state.gov.
5. Request deeds, court orders, DD-214, and immigration documents from the agency that controls the record.

## Common mistakes

- Paying unofficial sites for Social Security card replacement.
- Ordering uncertified copies that banks, courts, or passport offices reject.
- Starting with sentimental records while state ID and vital records are still missing.
- Assuming a photocopy of a will has the same effect as the original.
- Waiting on green card or work authorization replacement until employment is threatened.
- Forgetting custody, adoption, or divorce decrees until a school or medical deadline hits.

## Before it happens, do this once
Scan every Tier 1 legal document, write down the issuing agency for each, and store certified-copy instructions in the recovery binder.
