# FEMA Individual Assistance

## If you only read one line
Apply only when Individual Assistance is declared, use DisasterAssistance.gov or 1-800-621-FEMA (3362), and read every FEMA letter as a request for action, not as a final insult.
FEMA Individual Assistance is limited.
It helps with serious disaster-caused needs.
It does not make you whole.
It does not replace insurance.
It does not pay twice for the same loss.
It can still keep you housed, moving, and able to recover.

## What FEMA Individual Assistance is
FEMA Individual Assistance is federal aid for eligible people and households after a Presidential disaster declaration that authorizes Individual Assistance for your county, parish, borough, territory, or Tribal area.
If the declaration authorizes only Public Assistance, roads and public infrastructure may get help, but households may not.
Check:
- DisasterAssistance.gov
- FEMA.gov/disaster
- your state emergency management agency
- local emergency management notices
FEMA assistance is for uninsured or underinsured losses.
If insurance covers the need, FEMA cannot duplicate that benefit.
If insurance denies part of the claim, delays payment, or leaves a gap, FEMA may ask for the insurance settlement or denial letter.
FEMA can help renters and homeowners.
Most housing help is tied to the primary residence.
Secondary homes and vacation homes are not the target.

## Who is eligible
Eligibility depends on the program and the disaster, but the core screens are consistent:

- The damage was caused by the declared disaster.
- The damaged home was in the declared area.
- The damaged home was your primary residence.
- FEMA can verify your identity.
- FEMA can verify occupancy, and ownership if you ask for owner repair help.
- You or a household member meet citizenship or eligible immigration-status rules.
- Insurance or other aid does not cover the need.
Do not self-deny.
Apply if you have a disaster-caused serious need and the declaration covers you.
Let FEMA decide.

## How to apply
Apply online first if you can:

```text
https://www.DisasterAssistance.gov
```
Call if you cannot apply online:

```text
1-800-621-FEMA (3362)
```
You can also apply or get help at a Disaster Recovery Center when one is open.
Use the FEMA App if that is the easiest working option.
Before you apply, gather:
- Social Security number for you, another adult, or a minor child in the household
- pre-disaster address
- current address
- phone number
- email address
- insurance company name
- insurance policy number
- agent name if known
- household income
- bank routing and account number if using direct deposit
- description of damage
- photos if available
- accessibility needs
- language or communication needs
Save the FEMA application number.
Put it on the first page of the recovery binder.
Use the same name spelling and address format on every form.
Update FEMA if your phone, email, or mailing address changes.

## What FEMA may cover
FEMA's Individuals and Households Program has two broad buckets.

### Housing Assistance
Housing Assistance may include:
- repair money for a disaster-damaged owner-occupied primary residence
- rental assistance for temporary housing
- lodging expense reimbursement when eligible
- replacement assistance for a destroyed primary residence
- direct temporary housing in some disasters
- Permanent Housing Construction in rare cases where FEMA authorizes it
Repair money is for safe, sanitary, functional, and accessible housing.
It is not a remodel budget.
It may not restore the home to pre-disaster condition.

### Other Needs Assistance
Other Needs Assistance may include disaster-caused:
- medical expenses
- dental expenses
- funeral expenses
- child care
- personal property
- transportation
- moving and storage
- critical needs
- clean and sanitize needs
- miscellaneous necessary expenses approved for the disaster
Some Other Needs Assistance depends on whether you can get an SBA disaster loan.
If FEMA tells you to apply for an SBA loan, do not ignore it.
Declining the loan, or being denied, can still matter for FEMA processing.

## What FEMA does not cover
FEMA does not cover:
- insured losses
- business losses
- luxury upgrades
- normal wear and tear
- damage outside the declared incident period
- secondary homes
- vacation homes
- losses unrelated to the disaster
- the same need already paid by another source
Business losses go to SBA disaster loans, business insurance, tax records, and state programs.
FEMA grants usually do not have to be repaid.
But duplicate benefits can be recouped.
Do not spend money marked for a specific need on something else.

## The inspection process
FEMA may inspect your home.
The inspector verifies damage.
The inspector does not decide the final award at the door.
Have ready:
- FEMA application number
- photo ID if available
- proof of occupancy
- proof of ownership if you own
- insurance information
- photos of damage before cleanup
- receipts for temporary repairs
- contractor estimates if available
- list of damaged rooms
- list of damaged essential personal property
- accessibility or disability-related damage
Proof of occupancy can include:
- utility bill
- lease
- bank statement
- pay stub
- school document
- government benefit letter
- written statement if FEMA accepts it for the disaster
Proof of ownership can include:
- deed
- mortgage statement
- property tax bill
- title
- will or heirship documents
- manufactured home title
- structural insurance document

### Manufactured homes
For manufactured homes, have:
- title or registration if available
- lot lease if rented land
- serial or HUD label photos if accessible
- tie-down and foundation damage photos
- skirt, utility, and access damage photos
- park manager contact if relevant
Manufactured homes can hide damage under the unit.
Photograph skirting, piers, tie-downs, stairs, ramps, utility connections, and belly wrap.

### Site-built homes
For site-built homes, show:
- roof
- attic if safe
- crawlspace if safe
- foundation
- electrical panel
- HVAC
- water heater
- sewer or septic issues
- access routes if private road or bridge damage blocks entry
Do not climb, crawl, or enter unsafe areas for an inspection.
Tell the inspector what is inaccessible and why.

## The determination letter
FEMA sends a determination letter.
Read all pages.
Many apparent denials are requests for more information.
Look for:
- eligible amount
- reason code
- missing document
- identity issue
- insurance issue
- occupancy issue
- ownership issue
- inspection issue
- appeal instructions
- deadline
Common fixable problems:
- FEMA could not verify identity.
- FEMA could not verify occupancy.
- FEMA could not verify ownership.
- Insurance documents are missing.
- Damage was not visible at inspection.
- The address was entered wrong.
- You moved and missed contact.
- You did not answer the inspector.
Call FEMA if the letter is unclear.
Then respond in writing or upload documents through your account.
Keep proof of upload or mailing.

## Appeals
The usual appeal window is 60 days from the date on the FEMA decision letter.
Do not count from the day you opened the mail.
Count from the letter date.
Your appeal should be specific.
Include:
- your full name
- FEMA application number
- disaster number
- damaged property address
- current mailing address
- phone number
- the decision you are appealing
- what you believe is wrong
- what amount or help you are requesting
- documents that prove it
Useful attachments:
- insurance denial or settlement
- contractor estimate
- repair invoice
- photos
- lease
- utility bill
- deed
- title
- occupancy statement
- medical bill
- funeral bill
- vehicle repair estimate
- school or employer letter
Use one appeal per issue if that keeps it clear.
Label every attachment.
Keep a copy.
Mail with tracking or upload through the FEMA account.
If you use a Disaster Recovery Center, ask staff to scan and confirm receipt.
A legal-aid attorney helps by matching your evidence to the FEMA reason code, drafting the appeal, and spotting deadline or duplicate-benefit problems.
That is different from vague advice.
Use legal aid when the amount matters, the letter is confusing, or proof is hard because documents were destroyed.

## Recertification for continued rental assistance
Initial rental assistance can run out before your housing problem is fixed.
If you still cannot return home, ask FEMA about Continued Temporary Housing Assistance.
You may need to show:
- the first rental assistance was used for rent
- current lease or rental agreement
- rent receipts
- ongoing need
- home is still unsafe, inaccessible, or unavailable
- insurance does not cover the same housing need
- no other housing program is paying the same cost
- permanent housing plan
- progress toward that plan
Do not wait until the last day.
Ask for the continued assistance form as soon as you know you still need help.
Call:

```text
1-800-621-FEMA (3362)
```

## Keep FEMA separate from insurance
FEMA and insurance talk to different rules.
Insurance pays under a policy.
FEMA pays limited disaster assistance under federal rules.
Keep separate tabs in the binder.
If insurance later pays for a need FEMA already paid, tell FEMA.
Duplicate benefits can create repayment problems.

## 5 most important actions

1. Confirm Individual Assistance is declared for your area before applying.
2. Apply at DisasterAssistance.gov or call 1-800-621-FEMA (3362).
3. Save the FEMA application number and every letter in the binder.
4. Upload missing documents fast and keep proof of submission.
5. File any appeal within 60 days of the letter date.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a FEMA denial letter means final denial without reading the reason code.
- Missing the inspector's calls after changing phone numbers or shelter locations.
- Failing to file insurance first when insurance exists.
- Applying with inconsistent address or name formats that slow identity checks.
- Spending rental assistance without keeping rent receipts.
- Ignoring an SBA loan step and blocking other possible aid.

## Before it happens, do this once
Put proof of identity, proof of occupancy, proof of ownership or lease, insurance declarations, and a household income note in the recovery binder and cloud folder.
