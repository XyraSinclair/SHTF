# Housing Displacement

## If you only read one line
Register your location, update your mail, keep every housing receipt, and do not sign a long lease or cash deal while the ground is still moving.
Displacement turns one disaster into ten.
You lose your bed.
Then you miss mail.
Then the school calls.
Then insurance wants receipts.
Then the pet has nowhere to go.
Stabilize the address problem first.

## First 72 hours shelter
Use the safest available option:
- family
- friends
- Red Cross shelter
- local government shelter
- hotel
- faith-community shelter
- hospital social work placement if medical needs require it
Red Cross disaster help:

```text
1-800-RED-CROSS
1-800-733-2767
```
FEMA help after a declared disaster:

```text
DisasterAssistance.gov
1-800-621-FEMA (3362)
```
Document where you sleep each night.
Write:
- date
- address
- who stayed there
- cost
- receipt location
- reason you could not stay home
If you stay with family for free, document it anyway.
The insurer or FEMA may ask where you were and when.
If you pay family, use traceable payment and a written note.

## Address changes
Displacement creates missed mail.
Missed mail creates missed deadlines.
Do this in the first 72 hours if possible:
- temporary USPS mail forwarding
- insurer mailing address update
- FEMA mailing address update
- bank and credit-card mailing address update
- employer update
- school update
- pharmacy update
- medical provider update
- court or probation update if applicable
- benefits office update
Take screenshots.
Print confirmations.
Put them in the binder.
Use one stable mailing address if you can.
An out-of-area family mailbox can be better than a hotel that changes next week.

## FEMA Transitional Sheltering Assistance
Transitional Sheltering Assistance, or TSA, pays participating hotels directly in some disasters.
It is not automatic in every declaration.
You must register with FEMA first.
You must be eligible for the disaster and area.
Check:
- FEMA texts or emails
- FEMA App
- DisasterAssistance.gov account
- Disaster Recovery Center
- FEMA helpline at 1-800-621-FEMA (3362)
If approved:
- use a participating hotel
- confirm what taxes and fees are covered
- ask whether pets are allowed
- ask what checkout date FEMA lists
- keep incidental charges separate
- save receipts even if FEMA pays room and tax
TSA can end before your long-term housing is solved.
Do not wait until checkout morning to ask about next steps.

## Insurance ALE / Loss of Use
Additional Living Expenses, or ALE, is usually your insurance policy benefit for extra costs when a covered loss makes your home unfit to live in.
Ask your insurer:
- is ALE open
- what is the limit
- what is the time limit
- what housing needs pre-approval
- whether hotel taxes are covered
- whether pet boarding is covered
- whether storage is covered
- whether mileage is covered
- how food overage is calculated
- whether rent deposits are covered
ALE usually covers extra cost over normal spending.
Example:
- normal groceries: keep your normal baseline
- restaurant meals: claim the extra amount caused by displacement
- laundry: claim laundromat costs if you lost your washer
- mileage: claim extra commuting caused by the temporary location
Keep:
- itemized hotel receipts
- lease
- rent receipts
- restaurant receipts
- grocery receipts
- laundry receipts
- storage receipts
- pet boarding receipts
- mileage log
- parking receipts
- toll receipts
Do not rely on credit-card statements alone.
They rarely show what was bought.

## Short-term rentals after a disaster
The safest rental is boring:
- written lease
- legal owner or property manager
- traceable payment
- clear start and end date
- clear utility terms
- pet terms
- parking terms
- deposit terms
- cancellation terms
Prefer month-to-month or short terms until insurance and FEMA timelines are clearer.
Avoid:
- cash-only rentals
- no written lease
- pressure to sign a year lease immediately
- owner cannot prove authority
- price jumps after you show insurance papers
- no address on the lease
- no receipt for deposit
- "FEMA will reimburse this" promises from landlord
Many states activate price-gouging rules during emergency declarations.
If rent spikes sharply after the disaster, check your state attorney general or consumer protection agency.
Document the advertised price, date, contact, and quote.

## SBA disaster loans for displaced homeowners
SBA disaster home loans can help eligible homeowners repair or replace a primary residence.
SBA can also fund mitigation improvements in some cases.
For renters and homeowners, SBA may help replace personal property.
A loan may help when insurance, FEMA, and cash do not cover a safe housing plan.
But a loan is still debt.
Before accepting:
- compare payment to the rebuilt budget
- confirm eligible uses
- confirm disbursement timing
- confirm collateral if required
- confirm whether insurance proceeds must reduce the loan
- confirm what happens if you later receive more insurance money

## USPS mail forwarding
Start temporary mail forwarding fast.
Use official USPS Change of Address channels.
Avoid search-ad sites that charge extra for a simple change of address.
Save the confirmation.
Forward mail for:
- every adult
- children if they receive benefits, school mail, or medical mail
- business name if home business mail was delivered there
Forwarding does not update every sender.
You still need to change your address directly with banks, insurers, FEMA, courts, benefits, and medical providers.

## School continuity
Disaster displacement can make a student homeless under the McKinney-Vento Act.
That can protect school stability.
Students may have the right to stay in the school of origin when it is in the child's best interest.
Transportation may be required.
Call the district's McKinney-Vento liaison.
Use those words.
Say:
- "We are displaced because of a disaster."
- "We need McKinney-Vento support."
- "We want school-of-origin review."
- "We need transportation help."
- "We need records, meals, devices, uniforms, or supplies."
Document:
- date displaced
- old address
- current temporary address
- FEMA or shelter registration if any
- school calls
- transportation plan
Do not pull a child from school for weeks because housing is unsettled.
School routine is recovery infrastructure.

## Pets during displacement
Pets complicate shelter.
Plan early.
Options:
- pet-friendly hotel
- family or friend
- boarding kennel
- veterinary hospital boarding
- animal shelter disaster boarding
- foster network
- co-located shelter when available
Ask shelters before arrival:
- Are pets allowed in the same building?
- Are they housed separately?
- What records are required?
- Are carriers required?
- Are crates available?
- Are there species limits?
- Who feeds and walks them?
Keep:
- vaccination records
- microchip number
- medication list
- food
- leash
- carrier
- litter
- photo with owner
Many people re-enter unsafe zones for pets.
That kills people.
Plan pet housing before you are desperate.

## Special needs housing
If anyone needs oxygen, dialysis, wheelchair access, refrigeration for medication, electricity for equipment, or low-stimulation space, say it on every housing call.
Do not let the need stay implied.
Write:
- equipment
- power need
- medication storage
- mobility need
- bathroom need
- transportation need
- caregiver need
Ask FEMA, Red Cross, local emergency management, health department, and medical suppliers for functional-needs support.

## 5 most important actions

1. Record every sleeping location and cost from the first night onward.
2. Register with FEMA if Individual Assistance is declared and ask about TSA.
3. Open ALE with the insurer and keep itemized receipts.
4. Start USPS forwarding and direct address updates within 72 hours.
5. Call the school McKinney-Vento liaison if children are displaced.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a hotel bill is reimbursable without asking FEMA or insurance first.
- Losing mail and missing claim, court, benefit, or creditor deadlines.
- Signing a long lease before insurance, FEMA, or repair timelines are known.
- Paying cash for a rental deposit without a lease or receipt.
- Forgetting pet housing until the shelter door says no.
- Pulling children out of school routine instead of asking for McKinney-Vento help.

## Before it happens, do this once
Write down two out-of-area mailing addresses, pet-friendly hotels, school liaison contacts, and disability-related housing needs in the recovery binder.
