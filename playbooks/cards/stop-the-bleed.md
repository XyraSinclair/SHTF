# Stop the Bleed

**Uncontrolled bleeding kills in minutes. Direct pressure is the first step, but for severe limb bleeding a tourniquet is essential and should not be delayed.**

## Immediate response

1. **Call 911.** If others present, one person calls, one treats.
2. **Scene safe.** Move patient away from active danger first if needed.
3. **Gloves** if available. Treat all blood as infectious.
4. **Expose the wound.** Cut or tear clothing. You can't treat what you can't see.
5. **Identify the source.** Steady flow = venous. Spurting / pulsing = arterial.

## Step 1: Direct pressure

- Pack any clean cloth, gauze, or cloth directly into the wound.
- Press **hard**. Harder than feels polite.
- Do **not** lift to check. Lifting undoes clotting.
- Hold **10+ minutes** without interruption.
- Add cloth on top if bleeding through; do not replace cloth beneath.

## Step 2: Hemostatic gauze (if available)

If direct pressure isn't stopping it and you have QuikClot Combat Gauze, Celox, or similar:

1. Pack the gauze **directly into the wound**, following the source of bleeding.
2. Apply firm pressure on top for **3 minutes continuous**.
3. Reassess. If still bleeding, pack more.
4. Leave in place. Do not remove until medical care takes over.

## Step 3: Tourniquet (limbs only)

**If bleeding is severe and not controlled by pressure, apply a tourniquet immediately. Do not delay trying other methods.**

1. Place tourniquet **2–3 inches above the wound** (proximal, toward the heart). Not on a joint — above it.
2. Pull strap tight, apply through friction buckle.
3. Twist windlass until bleeding **stops completely**. It will hurt — the patient will react. Tighten anyway.
4. Secure windlass.
5. **Mark the time.** Use the tourniquet's time strap if present. Otherwise use a permanent marker on tape on the patient's forehead, or write directly on the forehead. 24-hour format: `T 14:37`.
6. **Do not remove.** Medical personnel only.

### Real tourniquets only

- CAT (Combat Application Tourniquet) — gold standard.
- SOF-T Wide — also good.
- Improvised: belt + windlass made of sturdy object (screwdriver, pen). Only as last resort. Most improvised tourniquets fail.
- **The stretchy "survival bracelets"** and keychain tourniquets are not effective. Don't trust them.

### Tourniquet myths

- **"Tourniquets cost the limb."** Up to 2 hours has very high salvage rates in trauma literature. Uncontrolled arterial bleeding kills in minutes.
- **"Loosen periodically."** No. Once applied, leave until surgical team takes over.
- **"Not until nothing else works."** Apply early for severe limb bleeding. Don't waste minutes trying escalating pressure.

## Step 4: Junctional bleeding (shoulder, groin, neck)

Tourniquet cannot be applied. Options:

- Hemostatic gauze **packed into the wound**, aggressive pressure above.
- Specialized junctional tourniquets (rare outside military/EMS).
- Expect loss; transport immediately.

## Penetrating chest wound (sucking chest wound)

A hole through the chest wall lets air into the pleural space on inhalation — collapses the lung, can progress to tension pneumothorax and death.

1. **Commercial vented chest seal** (Halo, HyFin) directly over the wound. Check the back — exit wound? Seal that too.
2. **Improvised** if no seal: a piece of non-porous plastic (bag, wrapper, glove) extending at least 2 inches past the wound on every side. Tape down on **three sides only** — leave the side closest to the ground untaped to vent outward. Flutter valve: air escapes, doesn't re-enter.
3. Sit the patient up if conscious and not in shock. Transport urgently.
4. Signs of tension pneumothorax (rising distress, one side of chest not moving, trachea shifting, jugular veins bulging) = lift or burp the seal to release pressure.

## Hard signs of severe hemorrhage

Any of these = immediate aggressive intervention:

- Spurting blood from a wound.
- Blood pooling rapidly on the ground.
- Bleeding through dressings within minutes.
- Patient confused, pale, clammy.
- Amputation or near-amputation.

## Shock management

Alongside bleeding control:

- Lay patient flat.
- Keep warm (body heat is heat, blanket if available).
- No food, no water.
- Elevate uninjured legs if no spinal injury.
- Reassure the patient.

## Positioning

- **Conscious**: sitting or lying, as they prefer; bleeding wound elevated if limb.
- **Unconscious, breathing**: recovery position (side, top leg bent forward).
- **Unconscious, not breathing**: CPR.

## Hand-off to EMS

Brief report:

- "Patient has a **[type] wound to the [location]**."
- "Major bleeding was controlled with **[pressure / hemostatic / tourniquet]**."
- "Tourniquet applied at **[time]**."
- "Loss of consciousness: **yes/no**. CPR: **yes/no**."
- "Known conditions / meds / allergies: **[if known]**."

## A note on nosebleeds

Not Stop-the-Bleed severity. Lean forward, pinch soft part of nose 10 full minutes without checking. If persists >30 minutes or you're on blood thinners, seek care.

---

## Practice

Every adult in the household should:

1. Take a free **Stop the Bleed** course. 90 minutes. Offered at hospitals, fire stations, community centers.
2. Apply a tourniquet to themselves (no tightening — just the motion) under stress once.
3. Have a tourniquet in their car and go-bag. Not in a backpack pocket that requires digging.
