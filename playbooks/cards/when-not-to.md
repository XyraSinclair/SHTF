# When NOT To

**Negations save more lives than instructions. Most bad outcomes come from doing the obvious-seeming thing, or the right thing at the wrong time. Read the why.**

## Driving

- **Don't drive through flooded roads.** 6 inches of moving water pushes a small car; 12–18 inches floats most vehicles. The majority of flood deaths happen inside cars. Turn around, don't drown.
- **Don't drive into heavy wildfire smoke** (AQI > 300, or visibility under 1/4 mile). Zero visibility plus panicking drivers equals chain collisions. Shelter and filter instead.
- **Don't drive over downed power lines.** Assume every line is live. Stay 35+ feet back; a wet road or damp tire can complete the circuit.
- **Don't start an evacuation late.** Contraflow, gridlock, and fuel shortages turn the route into a trap. Leave early or shelter in place — the worst option is leaving halfway.

## Generators and combustion

- **Don't run a generator indoors or in a garage** — not even with the door open. Carbon monoxide is colorless, odorless, and lethal within minutes.
- **Don't site a generator within 20 feet** of any window, door, or vent. CO drifts back in on still air.
- **Don't back-feed the grid with a male-to-male "suicide cord."** It electrocutes linemen and starts house fires. Use a transfer switch or nothing.
- **Don't use a charcoal grill, camp stove, or propane heater indoors.** Same CO mechanism — they produce it silently while you sleep.
- **Don't use a gas oven as a space heater.** Burns oxygen, produces CO, and can leak raw gas if the flame goes out.

## Water and waste

- **Don't drink tap water you've stored yourself for more than 6 months without re-treating it first.** Bacteria re-colonize even sealed containers. Commercial sealed bottled water is fine well past this window.
- **Don't disinfect with scented, color-safe, or old bleach.** Scented adds toxins; bleach older than ~1 year has degraded to near-inert. Plain unscented, under a year.
- **Don't drink floodwater — even boiled.** Boiling doesn't remove fuel, pesticides, sewage, or industrial chemicals. Find a different source.
- **Don't flush during a sewer backflow** or when the sewer lift station has lost power. Waste backs up into your home through the lowest drain.
- **Don't open a swollen, bulging, or leaking canned food.** That's botulism pressure. A single taste can kill. Discard sealed.

## Electricity and fire

- **Don't touch anything metal near downed lines,** flooded basements, or a tree that contacted a wire. Fences, pipes, and wet ground carry current much farther than people expect.
- **Don't enter a previously flooded basement** until utility power is confirmed off at the main AND the water is gone. Submerged outlets energize the water.
- **Don't re-enter a burned building** until the fire department clears it — even if flames are out. Hidden smoldering in walls and attics restarts, and CO lingers.
- **Don't throw water on a grease fire.** Water flashes to steam and throws burning oil everywhere. Lid, baking soda, Class K extinguisher, or get out.
- **Don't spray water on solar-panel roofs during a fire.** Panels stay energized in daylight. Tell firefighters you have them so they can de-energize safely.

## After a quake or storm

- **Don't light candles, matches, or any flame** until you've confirmed there's no gas smell and no cracked lines. One spark in a gas-filled room ends the building.
- **Don't flip light switches if you smell gas.** The switch arc is an ignition source. Leave, then call the utility from outside.
- **Don't re-enter a damaged structure** after a quake until inspected. Aftershocks bring down already-weakened framing and masonry.
- **Don't use an elevator** after a quake or during a fire. Power fails mid-shaft; smoke fills the car.

## Medical

- **Don't move a person with suspected neck or back injury** unless fire, water, or collapse is imminent. Movement can convert a survivable injury into permanent paralysis.
- **Don't give food or water to someone** with severe bleeding, shock, or likely surgery. It complicates anesthesia and can cause aspiration.
- **Don't remove an impaled object.** It's often tamponading the bleed. Stabilize around it with bulky dressings and transport.
- **Don't start CPR** on a patient with a valid DNR, obvious death signs (rigor, lividity, decomposition, incompatible injury), or when the scene is unsafe. You become the next patient.
- **Don't give aspirin to a child with a fever** (Reye's syndrome) or to anyone actively bleeding. Acetaminophen instead.
- **Don't induce vomiting in a suspected poisoning** unless Poison Control (1-800-222-1222) tells you to. Caustics burn twice; hydrocarbons aspirate.

## Evacuation vs. shelter

- **Don't evacuate a tornado warning** if you aren't already in a vehicle on the road. A car is the worst place in a tornado. Basement or interior windowless room.
- **Don't evacuate from fallout** without a confirmed clean destination and a transit time under the dose threshold. 48 hours sheltered beats 2 hours driving through plume.
- **Don't evacuate from a chemical release.** Close up, shelter, shut off HVAC, seal gaps. The plume passes faster than you can drive clear.
- **Don't evacuate an active shooter event through an unsecured corridor.** Run if the path is known clear; otherwise hide, barricade, then fight as last resort.

## Search and rescue

- **Don't search for someone in unsafe terrain** — flood, fire, collapsed structure. You become a second victim. Call SAR with a last-known location and description.
- **Don't enter a confined space** (well, tank, trench, basement with unknown air) without a buddy, an air check, and a retrieval line. Most confined-space deaths are would-be rescuers.

## Communications and payment

- **Don't tie up voice lines** in a disaster. Text gets through when calls don't. One call to the out-of-area family hub.
- **Don't expect ATMs, cards, or Venmo** to work post-event. Keep cash in small bills — tens and twenties, not hundreds.
- **Don't trust unverified rumors** on social media in the first 24 hours. Official channels only (NWS, local EM, fire/police feeds).

## People

- **Don't leave pets behind** unless truly unable. A disproportionate number of secondary deaths are people returning to retrieve them.
- **Don't argue with someone in acute panic.** Assign a concrete, simple task — "hold this, watch the door" — purpose breaks the freeze.
- **Don't say "everything happens for a reason" or "at least..."** to someone in acute loss. Presence and water are enough; platitudes aren't.

---

**When you're about to do something in a disaster, ask: "Is this on the don't list?"**
