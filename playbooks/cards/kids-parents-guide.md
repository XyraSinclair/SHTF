# Kids and Parents Preparedness Guide

**If you only read one line: make emergency prep feel like practice, not apocalypse.**

## 1. Involve, don't terrify

**Frame it as practice.**
- Call it camping practice, safety practice, or superhero training.
- Teach one action at a time. Do not narrate the worst case.
- Say what the child can do: get low, hold the flashlight, meet at the mailbox.
- Keep the adult face calm. Kids read your body before your words.

**Keep it honest.**
- Do not promise nothing bad will happen.
- Say: "Bad things can happen. We practice so we know what to do."
- Answer the question asked. Stop there.

## 2. Comfort items are survival gear

**Pack the familiar.**
- Small toy.
- Family photo.
- Familiar snack.
- Tiny blanket, cloth, or shirt that smells like home.

**Use it on purpose.**
- Put one comfort item in the kid go-bag.
- Put one duplicate in the car bag if you have a car.
- Do not mock the comfort object. It is nervous-system gear.

## 3. Memorize one phone number

**Pick one number.**
- Use one parent's cell phone or one out-of-area family contact.
- Put it to a song, chant, or clapping rhythm.
- Practice in boring moments: breakfast, bath, car ride, bedtime.
- Test gently. Do not turn it into a quiz under pressure.

**Back it up.**
- Write the number on the kid's emergency card.
- Put the card in the backpack, go-bag, and jacket pocket.
- Add allergies, medicines, and caregiver names.

## 4. Three meeting spots

**Make them specific.**
- Inside home: "under the kitchen table" or "by Mom's bed."
- Outside home: "the big tree by the mailbox," not "outside."
- Neighborhood: "the front steps of the library," not "somewhere safe."

**Walk them.**
- Touch each spot with the child.
- Practice from bedrooms, yard, car, and school pickup.
- Tell the child who is allowed to pick them up.

## 5. Give every kid a job

**Make the job real.**
- Flashlight checker.
- Pet-leash holder.
- Snack counter.
- Door watcher.
- Sibling hand-holder.

**Match age to task.**
- Small child: hold one item.
- Elementary child: carry one light bag and recite the meeting spot.
- Tween: check batteries, radio, water bottles, or the pet kit.

**Use focus.**
- A job gives scared hands somewhere to go.
- Praise the action, not bravery.
- Rotate jobs during calm practice.

## 6. Drills, not lectures

**Use a household rule-of-thumb cadence.**
- Fire drill: monthly until everyone can do it cold; then keep practicing.
- Earthquake drill: twice a year.
- Power-out dinner: quarterly. Cook without grid power or eat no-cook food by flashlight.

**Keep drills tiny.**
- Five minutes beats one annual lecture.
- Start with the alarm sound.
- End at the meeting spot.
- Stop before kids melt down.

## 7. Psychological first aid for kids

**Look.**
- Check safety first.
- Look for quiet kids, not only crying kids.
- Look for cold, hunger, thirst, injury, and separation.

**Listen.**
- Get low.
- Use short sentences.
- Say what is true: "You are with me. We are safe in this room right now."
- Let silence be silence.

**Link.**
- Link to water, warmth, food, sleep, and a familiar adult.
- Link to a small task.
- Link to routine: snack, story, sleep, check the radio.
- Use `playbooks/cards/psychological-first-aid.md` when panic, freeze, grief, or delayed reactions show up.

## Kid go-bag checklist

- Comfort item.
- ID card with caregiver names and phone numbers.
- Familiar snack.
- Water.
- Whistle.
- Small flashlight.
- Photo of caregivers.
- Spare clothes.
