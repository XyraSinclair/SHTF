# Tonight

One sheet. One evening. No philosophy. If all you do is these eight things before bed, you are meaningfully more prepared than you were this afternoon.

---

## The eight

1. **Test every smoke alarm and CO alarm.** Press the button. If it chirps weakly or doesn't sound, replace the battery now. If you don't have a CO alarm and you have any fuel appliance (gas stove, furnace, water heater, fireplace, garage attached to house), put one on the shopping list for tomorrow. CO kills more people after storms than the storms do.

2. **Fill four containers with tap water.** Clean jugs, pitchers, pots — whatever you have. Lid them. Put them under the sink or in a closet. If the water goes off, you already have a few days for drinking and cooking. No filter, no store run.

3. **Find every flashlight and check it.** Phone flashlights count. Put one by the bed, one near the main door, one in the kitchen. If a bulb or battery is dead, note it. Candles are a house fire; skip them.

4. **Write two phone numbers on paper.** One out-of-area contact (a relative or friend in a different city — they become the hub if local cells go down), and one local person who would come for you. Put the paper in your wallet. Screenshot it to your phone too.

5. **Pick one meeting point.** Somewhere near home where your household goes if you can't get back inside — a neighbor's porch, a street corner, a specific tree. Tell everyone who lives with you. One sentence: "If something bad happens, we meet at ___."

6. **Locate the medications that matter.** Anyone in the house on insulin, oxygen, heart meds, seizure meds, psychiatric meds, blood thinners, inhalers? Write down where they live (fridge, nightstand, locked cabinet), how many days are on hand, and the next refill date. Do not move refrigerated meds, controlled substances, or meds in locked storage out of where they're supposed to be — just know where they are. If anyone depends on a powered device (CPAP, oxygen concentrator, nebulizer, powered wheelchair), note the battery runtime and where the charger is.

7. **Know where your shutoffs are.** Water main (usually near the street or in the basement), gas valve (at the meter — a quarter-turn wrench lives nearby), electrical panel. You don't have to shut anything off tonight. You just have to know where they are.

8. **Charge everything.** Phone, laptop, any battery bank you own. Plug in whatever's low. A charged phone at bedtime is worth more than a plan you didn't make.

---

## If something happens tonight

The default is: **stay home unless staying is unsafe or officials order you to leave.** Fire in the building, flooding inside the house, smoke you can smell, or an evacuation order — those override. Everything else — stay.

If someone is bleeding badly: press hard on the wound with anything clean. Keep pressing until help arrives or it stops.

If the power goes out: don't open the fridge. Don't run a generator indoors or in an attached garage (CO). Don't use a gas stove for heat.

---

## Tomorrow, not tonight

Go-bags, 14-day food, radios, generators, solar, the library — all of it can wait. Tonight is just these eight. Sleep.
