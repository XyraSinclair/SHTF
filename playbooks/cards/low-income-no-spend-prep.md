# Low-Income No-Spend Prep

**If you only read one line: start with containers, paper, people, and practice before buying gear.**

## 1. Water is free

**Fill what you already have.**
- Empty 2-L soda bottles.
- Juice jugs.
- Gallon water jugs.
- Food-grade containers with tight lids.

**Make it safe enough.**
- Wash with soap.
- Rinse well.
- Sanitize if you can.
- Fill from the tap while the tap is safe.
- Label with the date.
- Rotate home-filled water every 6 months.

**Do not use poison containers.**
- No bleach bottles.
- No pesticide bottles.
- No fuel or chemical jugs.

## 2. Shop your pantry

**Inventory first.**
- Pull everything onto the table.
- Count meals, not cans.
- Build a no-cook menu from what you already eat.
- Put the oldest food in front.

**You may have more than you think.**
- Many households can assemble several days, often a week or more, from pantry food, rice, pasta, oats, peanut butter, beans, and canned goods.
- Write the menu before buying anything.

## 3. Knowledge weighs nothing

**Use free sources while internet works.**
- Library books.
- FEMA PDFs.
- CDC PDFs.
- Red Cross PDFs.
- Local emergency management pages.
- Skill videos saved for offline use.
- This repo.

**Print the few pages that matter.**
- Water purification.
- First aid.
- Stop the bleed.
- Family phone numbers.
- Medication list.

## 4. The library is a prep resource

**Use the building.**
- Meeting point.
- Cooling center when designated.
- Heat relief in winter if open.
- Power outlets when available.
- Wi-Fi.
- Printers.
- Local information desk.

**Ask librarians.**
- Where is the food bank list?
- Where are cooling centers posted?
- Where are shelters posted?
- What local forms should be printed before an emergency?

## 5. Mutual aid is faster than agencies

**Know names before need.**
- Food bank.
- Community fridge.
- Church pantry.
- Mosque, synagogue, temple, or mutual-aid group.
- Neighborhood group.
- Tenant association.

**Make the exchange real.**
- Share rides.
- Share child care.
- Share tools.
- Share printed cards.
- Trade skills, not money.

## 6. The two cheapest buys that matter

**Unscented chlorine bleach.**
- Buy plain, unscented household bleach with 5% to 9% sodium hypochlorite.
- Use it for water disinfection only by CDC directions.
- Do not use scented, splashless, color-safe, or unknown-strength bleach for drinking water.

**Canning jars.**
- Use them for dry storage, leftovers, water, and preservation.
- Inspect rims.
- Do not reuse damaged lids for canning.
- Follow USDA canning directions exactly for shelf-stable food.

## 7. Skills that replace gear

**Learn what removes purchases.**
- Knots.
- Basic first aid.
- Fire-making where legal and safe.
- Map-reading.
- Mending.
- Cooking from dry staples.
- Cleaning and bandaging small wounds.

**Practice with what you have.**
- Tie knots with shoelaces.
- Read paper maps at the library.
- Sew a torn pocket.
- Cook a pantry dinner without opening the fridge.
- Walk the evacuation route.

---
*Source: Ready.gov "Low and No Cost Preparedness"; CDC "How to Create and Store an Emergency Water Supply" and "How to Make Water Safe in an Emergency"; USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning; American Red Cross make-a-plan and emergency preparedness materials; local food banks and libraries vary by community.*
