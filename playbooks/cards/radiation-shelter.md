# Radiation Shelter Card

**If fallout is coming: get inside, stay inside, stay tuned. 48–72 hours minimum.**

## The 7/10 rule (memorize this)

Fallout radiation drops fast in the first days:

| Time since detonation | Radiation level |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| 1 hour | 100% (reference) |
| 7 hours | 10% |
| 49 hours (~2 days) | 1% |
| 2 weeks | 0.1% |

The first 24–48 hours are by far the most dangerous. Shelter discipline then saves your life.

## Three protections, in order

1. **Distance** from the fallout (outside, on ground, above shelter).
2. **Shielding mass** between you and the fallout (earth, concrete, water, books).
3. **Time** — every hour inside is safer than the last.

## Best place to shelter (best to worst)

| Location | Protection factor |
|----------|-------------------|
| Deep basement, away from windows | 40–200× |
| Middle of a large multi-story building | 10–100× |
| Basement corner of single-family home | 10–40× |
| Interior room of ground floor, no windows | 3–10× |
| Single-story home near exterior walls | 2–5× |
| Mobile home | 1–3× |
| Vehicle | 1–2× |
| Outdoors | 1× (no protection) |

## Maximizing your shelter

### Mass

Pile any dense material between you and outer walls / roof:

- Books, wet sand, earth, concrete blocks, water-filled containers, brick.
- 4 inches of concrete ≈ 2 feet of packed earth ≈ 1 inch of lead.
- Fill bags (trash bags, duffel bags) with earth or sand and stack around your shelter position.

### Sealing

Close every opening. Seal gaps with:

- Duct tape + heavy plastic sheeting over windows, vents, door gaps.
- Damp cloth or rolled towels under doors.
- Duct tape in gaps of door frames.
- Shut HVAC; seal air returns.

### Shelter position

- Lowest floor.
- Interior corner.
- Farthest from external walls and roof above.
- Under a heavy desk or staircase adds shielding mass overhead.

## Expedient earth shelter (if no basement)

Trench shelter, built in 1–8 hours per NWSS Ch. 5:

1. Dig 3–4 ft deep trench, 3 ft wide, length for occupants.
2. Cover with any pole-like material (doors, lumber, saplings).
3. Cover poles with plastic (prevents dirt fall-through).
4. Heap 2+ ft of packed earth on top.
5. Leave an entry with a right-angle turn (baffles gamma radiation).

Two feet of packed earth blocks ~99% of initial gamma radiation.

## Supplies for the 72-hour shelter period

- **Water**: 4 L / person / day (more if possible).
- **Food**: any calories. Shelf-stable preferred.
- **Light**: headlamps, batteries.
- **Radio**: battery or hand-crank AM/FM/NOAA.
- **Sanitation**: bucket with lid, trash bags, sawdust/kitty litter.
- **Medical**: personal medications, basic first aid.
- **Clothing**: warm layers; shelter may be cool and poorly ventilated.

## During shelter

### Don't

- Don't go outside unless absolutely necessary.
- Don't open doors/windows.
- Don't drink from uncovered containers.
- Don't eat food that was uncovered outside.

### Do

- Listen to emergency broadcasts.
- Check yourself and others for symptoms (nausea, vomiting, weakness = possible radiation sickness).
- Ration water deliberately.
- Maintain morale: meals on schedule, light activity, rest.

## Decontamination (if forced outside)

Any exposure to fallout requires decon on re-entry:

1. Remove outer clothing outside the shelter. Bag and seal.
2. Wipe or brush off visible dust before entering.
3. Wash hands, face, exposed skin with soap and cool water.
4. Shower full-body if water permits. No hot water (opens pores).
5. Put on clean clothing.
6. Put removed clothing in sealed bag, far from living area.

## Potassium iodide (KI) — narrow use

- Protects **thyroid** against **iodine-131** only.
- Useful near **nuclear power plant accidents** releasing I-131.
- Less clearly useful for nuclear weapons (fallout composition differs).
- Not protection against blast, gamma, other isotopes, or fallout broadly.
- Dose: adults 130 mg/day, children 65 mg, infants 32 mg (verify with FDA/current guidance).
- Take **before or within hours of exposure**.
- Stop when exposure stops — chronic KI has side effects.

## Radiation sickness (rough guide)

| Exposure | Symptoms | Survival (with care) |
|----------|----------|---------------------|
| <50 rem | None acute | Full |
| 50–200 rem | Nausea, vomiting, fatigue | Very likely |
| 200–400 rem | Marked GI, immune suppression | 50–95% |
| 400+ rem | Severe GI, hematologic | Low |
| 1000+ rem | Massive multisystem | Near-zero |

Most civilians in shelter will receive doses in the lower ranges if shelter discipline is maintained.

## When to leave shelter

- After **48–72 hours** minimum in most scenarios.
- Gradual: brief outdoor activity first (~15 min), extending over days.
- Avoid visible accumulated fallout (gray dust).
- Follow authority guidance on exclusion zones.
- Do not eat food / drink water from uncovered sources for weeks after.

## After shelter

- Cover yourself when outdoors (long sleeves, hat).
- Minimize outdoor time first few weeks.
- Water from sealed / deep sources; filter + disinfect anything else.
- Food: cooked, covered, from sealed packaging.
- Wash hands frequently, especially before eating.
- Medical: seek care for any symptoms even if delayed.

## Before it happens, do this once

1. Identify your shelter position. Test access to it.
2. Know where your heaviest dense material is (stored books? sand? concrete blocks?) — could be staged quickly around shelter.
3. Stock 14-day food/water (see [`../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md`](../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md)).
4. Heavy plastic sheeting + duct tape in your kit (cheap, small, critical for sealing).
5. Read NWSS chapters 1, 5, 11 while you have time to read calmly.
