High-Signal SHTF Field Brief

Print landscape, cut in half, laminate if useful. This is an optional dense supplement, not the basic first-aid deck. Use it when the situation is messy, technical, or coordination-heavy.

If you only read one line: reduce variance. Water, information, sleep, neighbors, calories, and distance from chaos beat improvised theatrics.

0. Operating Doctrine

First 10 minutes

  1. Stop immediate hazards: fire, bleeding, CO, floodwater, violence, exposed wire.
  2. Account for people. Text status to out-of-area contact.
  3. Turn on AM/FM/NOAA. Start written log: time, source, fact, action.
  4. Charge phone, radio, battery banks. Fill water if utilities may fail.
  5. Pick default: stay unless a specific hazard makes staying worse.

Decision filters

  • Leave: fire, flood, structural collapse, official evacuation, heat/cold you cannot survive, targeted threat.
  • Stay: roads uncertain, violence elsewhere, supplies on hand, vulnerable people hard to move.
  • Delay: you are tired, angry, hungry, or information is single-source.
  • Write: the next decision, the reason, the next check time.

Daily rhythm

Morning: radio, weather, water, sanitation, check neighbors. Midday: heavy work, charging, repair, trade. Dusk: cook, lockup, CO/fire check, comms window, sleep plan. Night: no major decisions unless the hazard is physical and present.

Social default

Most people cooperate in disasters. Build that reality. Also assume hunger, withdrawal, fear, and opportunism can appear fast. Be generous in small units, private about reserves, and boringly clear about boundaries.

Sources: repo playbooks: stay-or-go, grid-down, what-kills, psychological-first-aid. General defaults; adapt for body, household, law, climate, and official instructions.

2. Baofeng UV-5R Minimums

Fast receive

  • VFO/MR: frequency mode vs memory mode.
  • A/B: select top line before entering freq.
  • Enter 162550 for NOAA WX7, or any freq without decimal.
  • Hold *SCAN to scan. # toggles key lock.
  • Use low power for local; high only when needed. Short transmissions.

Manual store

  • Delete channel: MENU 28 MENU ch MENU EXIT.
  • Simplex: set freq, MENU 25 OFF, then MENU 27 MENU ch MENU.
  • Repeater: set RX freq; MENU 13 tone; MENU 25 +/-; MENU 26 offset; store RX and TX with MENU 27.
  • Best: program with CHIRP before trouble.

3. Message Format

THIS IS [call/name]. PRIORITY [emergency/urgent/routine]. LOCATION [plain language + grid if known]. SITUATION [one sentence]. NEED [specific]. PEOPLE [number/status]. INTENT [stay/leave/route]. NEXT CONTACT [time/channel]. OVER.

Ham: identify with callsign every 10 min and at end. GMRS normally needs a license; a GMRS licensee may allow anyone to use that station for an emergency message. For amateur stations, FCC rules allow any available means for immediate safety-of-life/property protection when normal comms are unavailable.

Comms windows

Agree now: monitor at top of each hour for 5 minutes; use SMS first, then radio. Do not keep radios transmitting casually; batteries and attention are scarce.

2. Food And Garden Rules After Fallout

Safer first

  • Sealed pantry food, indoor water, covered tanks, deep wells if intact.
  • Food inside refrigerators/freezers if power and seals held.
  • Root crops only after guidance/testing; scrub/peel; discard wash water away from living area.
  • Greenhouses/high tunnels help only if closed before deposition.

Avoid until cleared

  • Fresh milk from grazing animals; I-131 and Sr-90 concentrate concern.
  • Leafy greens, herbs, berries, exposed mushrooms: high surface area.
  • Rain barrels, roof runoff, ponds, uncovered feed, open grain bins.
  • Ash or dust on fields: do not till it in as "fertilizer."

Decon logic

Outside dust comes off mechanically. Remove outer layer, bag it, wash exposed skin/hair with soap and cool water. Do not use conditioner. Keep contaminated shoes and tools out of sleeping/cooking space.

Gardening is resilience, not instant calories. Existing pantry beats a fantasy crop. For calories: potatoes, winter squash, dry beans, corn, grains, oil crops. For vitamins/morale: greens and herbs. For survival: water, soil fertility, storage, pest control, seed saving, and labor scheduling matter more than seed packets.

KI

Potassium iodide protects the thyroid against radioactive iodine only. It does not stop gamma, fallout dust, blast, Cs-137, Sr-90, or Pu. CDC says use KI only when instructed by officials or a clinician; FDA dosing is source-specific.

Loads to prioritize

Medical devices -> phone/radio -> lights -> battery banks -> laptop/local reference -> refrigeration only while food is worth saving -> comfort. A phone charge is small; a freezer and heater are not.

Generator discipline

Never indoors, garage, balcony, shed, or near open windows. CPSC says outside only, at least 20 ft from the house, exhaust away. Run in blocks: cool freezer, charge batteries, shut down. Log fuel in gallons, hours, and load.

What to ask

  • "Given this inventory, make a 7-day meal plan."
  • "Summarize this PDF section into a checklist."
  • "Calculate water/fuel needed for 4 people, 10 days."
  • "Draft a radio message under 25 words."
  • "Find contradictions between these two plans."

What not to trust

  • Diagnosis, medication dosing, radiation treatment, law, self-defense thresholds.
  • Anything not checked against local PDFs or official broadcasts.
  • Confident answers from vague prompts. Ask for assumptions and source path.

People, Trade, Security

Default: build cooperation early, avoid reputation damage, and keep enough boundary to survive people who do not reciprocate.

1. Social Reality

Good signs

  • People bring names, addresses, families, skills, and specific needs.
  • They accept small transactions and future check-in times.
  • They ask before entering, touching gear, or photographing.
  • They can hear "no" without escalation.

Bad signs

  • Urgency theater: "right now or else."
  • Intoxication, weapons handling, probing stash questions.
  • Large group sends one talker while others drift or flank.
  • They want credit, entry, secrecy, or your exact reserves.

2. Trade SOP

  1. Trade from a small pre-separated pouch, never from main storage.
  2. Use neutral visible ground. Avoid your doorway, vehicle trunk, or sleeping area.
  3. Small lots first. No debt unless you already trusted them before the event.
  4. State units aloud: "two liters treated water for four AA cells."
  5. Use reputation: names, house, who vouched, what happened. Write it down.
  6. End cleanly: "That is all I can spare today. Check again tomorrow at 1600."

High-value trade that does not expose your pantry

Charging phones, radio relay, tool repair, water treatment, printed maps, bicycle repair, can-opening, coffee/tea by the cup, hot water, information, labor, childcare, clean containers, batteries, tape, rope, salt, sugar, lighters. Do not trade prescription meds, ammunition, or anything that creates legal or retaliation risk.

3. Security Without Theater

  • Avoidance is the best self-defense. Distance beats dominance displays.
  • If moving/trading, one person moves; one trusted person knows route, ETA, radio channel, and what to do if overdue.
  • A legally carried defensive tool is private until needed. Brandishing, warning shots, and online tough talk create danger and legal exposure.
  • Doors stay locked. Conversations happen through a barrier or outside at distance.
  • Do not advertise generator, fuel, food, guns, cash, or that you are alone.
  • If you have an armed backup, the job is observation, comms, and extraction, not escalation.

Sleep, Work, Fill-In

Sleep is not softness. It is command capacity. After one bad night, decisions degrade; after several, people hallucinate patterns and start fights with reality.

1. Sleep Under Uncertainty

Set the site

  • Sleep away from windows and street-facing walls.
  • Shoes, headlamp, glasses, keys, radio, water within one arm.
  • Earplugs + eye cover if safe; one ear open if alone.
  • Insulate from ground. Cold floor steals sleep.
  • CO alarm active if any combustion or generator exists nearby.

Protect cognition

  • Two 4-hour blocks beat heroic all-nighters.
  • Buddy watches: 2-3 hours per shift; write handoff notes.
  • Caffeine early, not as a substitute for sleep.
  • No big arguments after dark. Park decisions until morning unless danger is immediate.
  • Kids and elders mirror adult calm. Routines are medicine.

3. Before It Happens, Do This Once

  1. Print this brief and the standard cards. Laminate. Put one in car, go-bag, kitchen.
  2. Program radio memories: NOAA, 146.520, 446.000, FRS/GMRS 1/20, MURS 1, two local repeaters.
  3. Run ./tools-scripts/get-squared-away.sh --full. Verify what is actually present.
  4. Open the local AI runner once: ./tools-scripts/run-gemma4-llamacpp.sh --quant Q4_K_M E2B.
  5. Measure your power: phone charge Wh, laptop Wh, battery bank Wh, generator fuel per hour, solar Wh/day.
  6. Mark Kindle/offline favorites: medical, water, sanitation, radio, maps, NWSS, canning, seed saving.
  7. Meet three neighbors before you need them. Know who has kids, elders, medical devices, tools, radios.
