# Older Adult Living Alone

**If you only read one line: your network is the prep that keeps the door from staying closed.**

## 1. Your network is your #1 prep

**Pick three keyholders minimum.**
- One neighbor.
- One friend.
- One relative or caregiver.
- Each person agrees before the emergency.

**Make it practical.**
- Exchange keys or lockbox codes.
- Show them the shutoffs, med list, go-bag, and pet supplies.
- Agree how they check on you if phones fail.
- Make the relationship mutual. You can check on them too.

## 2. Medical continuity

**Keep care from breaking.**
- Ask your prescriber and pharmacist how to build a 30-day prescription surplus where legal.
- Take a clear photo of every pill bottle label.
- Keep a laminated medication list in wallet, fridge, and go-bag.
- Write the prescriber's phone number on paper.

**Keep the list useful.**
- Diagnosis.
- Drug name.
- Dose.
- Time taken.
- Allergies.
- Pharmacy phone.
- Insurance card copy.

## 3. Shelter-in-place comfort kit

**Treat boredom as a risk.**
- Books.
- Large-print puzzles.
- Battery radio.
- Cards.
- Knitting, carving, drawing, or another quiet hobby.
- Paper calendar and pen.

**Keep body needs close.**
- Water near chair and bed.
- Shelf-stable food that opens without a hard grip.
- Warm layers.
- Spare eyeglasses.
- Hearing-aid batteries.

## 4. Simple, no-tech communication

**Use one out-of-area ICE contact.**
- Pick one person outside your region.
- Put the number on a wallet card.
- Tape the number near the landline or charging spot.
- Tell your network: "If local calls fail, report to this person."

**Do not trust the phone alone.**
- Keep numbers on paper.
- Keep a stamped envelope and notepad.
- Keep a whistle by the bed.
- If you have a radio, write the local emergency stations on the radio.

## 5. The "I'm OK" window sign

**Make the signal bright.**
- Green card up: okay.
- Card down or missing: check on me.
- Red card: need help now.

**Practice it.**
- Put the card where neighbors can see it.
- Agree on what it means before the event.
- Do a test check with the floor, block, or cul-de-sac.

## 6. Evacuation assistance arranged in advance

**Register before you need it.**
- County special-needs or access-and-functional-needs registry.
- Utility medical-baseline or life-support list if you use powered medical equipment.
- Building manager list if you live in senior housing or an apartment.

**Assign the ride.**
- One network member owns the first ride plan.
- One backup owns the second ride plan.
- Keep walker, cane, wheelchair, oxygen, meds, and chargers together.
- Do not wait until the order is already urgent.

## 7. Fall prevention matters more now

**Light the path.**
- Headlamp on the nightstand.
- Flashlight in bathroom.
- Night light in hall if power is up.
- Clear route from bed to toilet and exit.

**Keep feet safe.**
- Non-slip shoes.
- No loose slippers on stairs.
- No throw rugs in the main path.
- Cane or walker parked where your hand lands first.

**Assume dark plus stress.**
- Disasters often start at night.
- Hands need to stay free.
- Sit down before you stand if dizzy.

*Source: American Red Cross "Emergency Preparedness for Older Adults" and "Building Your Support Network"; Ready.gov older adults and people with disabilities preparedness guidance; CDC emergency preparedness for chronic disease and medical needs; MedlinePlus / NIH fall prevention guidance.*
