# Renter Preparedness

**If you only read one line: make your system portable, insured, and tied to the building you actually live in.**

## 1. Portable over permanent

**Buy nothing you cannot move.**
- Power station beats generator in an apartment.
- Water filter beats fixed tank.
- Collapsible water jugs beat 55-gallon drums.
- Door wedges, flashlights, radio, first aid, and go-bag beat built-in projects.

**Respect shared risk.**
- No fuel-burning generator indoors, in a garage, on a balcony, or near windows.
- Do not store illegal fuel or propane where your lease or fire code forbids it.
- Keep batteries, chargers, and cords off piles of bedding and paper.

## 2. Know your lease

**Read it before the event.**
- Insurance requirement.
- Alteration rules.
- Balcony and fuel rules.
- Guest and pet rules.
- Emergency notification responsibilities.

**Write the useful parts.**
- Landlord emergency number.
- Property manager number.
- Maintenance portal and after-hours line.
- Where notices are posted.
- How the building sends urgent messages.

## 3. Renters insurance is not optional

**Cover the stuff and the displacement.**
- Renters insurance often costs around $15-25/month.
- It covers personal property under covered losses.
- It can include additional living expenses, also called loss-of-use.
- Loss-of-use is the hotel, temporary rental, and extra meal money when your unit is uninhabitable after a covered loss.

**Make the claim possible.**
- Photo every room.
- Photo serial numbers.
- Save receipts for expensive items.
- Keep the policy number on paper and in your phone.

## 4. Go-bag is your primary system

**Do not build around a basement stockpile.**
- Many renters move often.
- Fire, flood, condemnation, and lockout all push you out fast.
- Your first system is the bag you can carry down stairs.

**Pack for the building.**
- Shoes by bed.
- Headlamp.
- N95 or better mask for smoke and dust.
- Gloves.
- Copies of lease, ID, insurance, and pet records.
- Cash in small bills.

## 5. Know the building shutoffs

**Find them now.**
- Main water shutoff.
- Unit water shutoff if there is one.
- Gas meter or building gas valve.
- Electrical panel.
- Fire alarm pull stations.
- Exit stairs.

**Know authority.**
- Ask who has the key.
- Ask when tenants may touch a shutoff.
- Do not shut off gas unless you smell gas, hear gas, see damage, or authorities tell you.
- If you shut gas off, let the utility turn it back on.

## 6. Absentee landlord plan

**Decide the 2 a.m. call.**
- Burst pipe.
- Gas smell.
- Locked exit.
- No heat in dangerous cold.
- Sparking outlet.

**Keep the paper trail.**
- Write the call log.
- Photograph damage.
- Save hotel and repair receipts.
- Keep a blank certified-mail demand-letter template with your lease file.
- Use local legal aid or tenant union templates for your state.

## 7. Floor captains and community

**One floor beats one stockpile.**
- One person per floor keeps a check-on-neighbors list.
- One person keeps a walkie-talkie or building radio plan.
- One person knows who uses oxygen, wheelchair, cane, service animal, or needs extra time.

**Make it consent-based.**
- Ask before listing medical or access needs.
- Keep the list private.
- Practice the stairwell and meeting point.
- Check on elderly neighbors, new parents, disabled neighbors, and anyone who lives alone.
