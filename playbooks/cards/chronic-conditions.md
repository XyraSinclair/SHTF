# Chronic Conditions in a Disaster

**About 40% of US adults live with a chronic condition. In extended grid-down, evacuation, or pandemic events, most excess deaths on days 3–14 are people whose routine care was interrupted. Treat meds, power, and cold chain as life-safety gear.**

## 1. Insulin and diabetes

**Keep it functional.**
- Unopened insulin: refrigerated until manufacturer date. Unrefrigerated insulin is typically stable ~28 days at room temp below 86°F (30°C).
- Cooler with ice packs — insulin must stay **cool, not frozen**. Freezing destroys insulin permanently. Wrap vials; no direct contact with ice.
- Keep fast sugar (glucose tabs, juice, hard candy) within arm's reach.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- **DKA**: fruity/acetone breath, deep rapid (Kussmaul) breathing, nausea, abdominal pain, confusion → 911.
- **Severe hypo**: unable to swallow, seizing, unresponsive → glucagon IM/nasal, recovery position, 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- 30-day Rx surplus. Test strips, lancets, ketone strips, glucagon if hypo-prone.
- Hard-copy Rx list + prescriber contact. Insulin-pen backup for pump users.

## 2. Oxygen therapy

**Keep it functional.**
- Continuous concentrators pull ~300–600W. Most portable gas generators handle it; most small solar "power stations" cannot run one for long.
- Portable O2 concentrator on battery + backup compressed tanks is the grid-down answer.
- Rough tank duration at 2 LPM: **E tank ~5 hrs; M60 ~16 hrs**.
- No smoking, no open flame, no petroleum grease anywhere near O2.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- New confusion, cyanosis (blue lips/fingertips), severe air hunger, inability to speak full sentences → 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Call your O2 supplier for a disaster plan and backup-tank policy.
- Register on the utility's medical-equipment priority list and life-support list.

## 3. Dialysis

**Keep it functional.**
- Hemodialysis patients have roughly a **72-hour life-threatening window** without treatment.
- Emergency renal diet while access is interrupted: **low potassium** (no bananas, oranges, potatoes, tomatoes, melons), **low sodium**, strict fluid limit per your nephrologist's number.
- Take Kayexalate / patiromer only if prescribed.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Rapid weight gain, swelling, chest pain, severe shortness of breath, irregular heart rhythm → 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Your center's disaster plan in writing. Addresses of 2+ backup centers in likely evacuation directions.
- Carry a dialysis ID card and current med list. Know your dry weight.

## 4. Heart medications

**Keep it functional.**
- **Beta blockers** (metoprolol, carvedilol, atenolol): never stop abruptly — rebound hypertension and MI risk.
- **Anticoagulants** (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran): missed doses = clot risk; doubled doses = bleed risk. Keep a **14-day surplus minimum**.
- **Nitroglycerin**: light- and heat-sensitive. Replace ~6 months after opening the bottle. Store in original amber glass.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- **Stroke — FAST**: Face droop, Arm drift, Speech slurred, Time to call 911. Note last-known-well time.
- **MI**: pressure/pain in chest, sweat, shortness of breath, jaw/arm pain → chew one 325 mg aspirin (unless allergic or told not to), 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- BP cuff you know how to use. Written target BP and heart rate.

## 5. Asthma / COPD

**Keep it functional.**
- **Two rescue inhalers minimum** (albuterol). Store between 59–86°F — never in a hot car.
- Nebulizer + battery inverter for severe asthmatics and pediatric patients.
- **Controller meds** (ICS, LABA, LAMA, biologics) are not skippable — they prevent the attacks you can't treat in a disaster.
- Wildfire smoke or dust: **N95/KN95**, seal one clean room (tape plastic over vents), run a HEPA filter if powered.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Cannot speak a full sentence, rescue inhaler not working, cyanosis, drowsiness, silent chest → 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Written asthma/COPD action plan from your MD. Peak flow meter if you use one.

## 6. CPAP / sleep apnea

**Keep it functional.**
- Untreated severe OSA drives strokes, arrhythmia, and cardiac events — exactly when you most need to function.
- DC adapter + deep-cycle battery, or a commercial CPAP battery pack. Most CPAPs run ~30–60Wh/hr without humidifier.
- Water chamber: **distilled or disinfected water only**. Run without humidifier before using tap.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Morning headaches, chest pain, new arrhythmia, daytime confusion after missed nights → seek care.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- SD card backup of machine settings. Spare mask, hose, filters.

## 7. Anaphylaxis / epinephrine

**Keep it functional.**
- **Two EpiPens (or equivalent) minimum**, on-person where possible.
- Heat-sensitive — never leave in a car. Cloudy or discolored = degraded.
- **Expired epi is preferred over no epi** for true anaphylaxis.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Hives + airway/breathing/GI/cardiovascular symptoms = anaphylaxis. Inject mid-outer thigh, lay flat, legs up, 911.
- After injection: 911 regardless — biphasic rebound occurs in up to ~20% within hours.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Diphenhydramine (adjunct, not replacement). Written allergen list and action plan.

## 8. Seizure medications

**Keep it functional.**
- **Never skip AEDs** (levetiracetam, lamotrigine, valproate, etc.). Abrupt withdrawal triggers breakthrough seizures and status.
- Rescue benzo (rectal diazepam, nasal midazolam) per neurologist's plan.
- Sleep deprivation, dehydration, and missed meals lower the threshold — protect all three.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Seizure >5 minutes, or back-to-back without recovery = **status epilepticus** → 911.
- First-ever seizure, seizure with injury, pregnancy, or in water → 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Medical alert ID. Written seizure action plan. 30-day AED surplus.

## 9. Mental-health medications

**Keep it functional.**
- **SSRIs, SNRIs, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers**: missed doses risk delayed relapse (days–weeks out). Don't stop.
- **Benzodiazepines**: never stop abruptly — withdrawal seizures and delirium. Taper only under MD.
- **Lithium**: dehydration drives toxicity. Hydrate aggressively in heat; watch for tremor, confusion, vomiting.
- **Clozapine / MAOIs / stimulants**: special risks — keep the prescriber's number reachable.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- Suicidal intent with plan, psychosis, lithium toxicity signs, benzo withdrawal tremor → 911 or 988.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- 30-day surplus. Prescriber phone. Sleep plan — sleep loss is a relapse trigger.

## 10. Pregnancy

**Keep it functional.**
- Continue prenatal vitamins. Hydrate. Keep moving within tolerance. Avoid known teratogens and unfiltered water.
- After 32 weeks, or any high-risk pregnancy: **relocate to care before** the event if possible.

**When it becomes emergency.**
- **Pre-eclampsia**: severe headache, vision changes, right-upper-quadrant pain, rapid face/hand swelling → 911.
- Vaginal bleeding, leaking fluid, decreased fetal movement, regular contractions before 37 weeks, seizure → 911.

**Pre-disaster prep.**
- Copy of prenatal records. OB and L&D phone numbers. Hospital bag packed from 34 weeks.

## Closing essentials

- **Medical alert tag or bracelet** — wear it. First responders read wrists and necks first.
- **Utility special-needs / medical-baseline list** — call the power company and water utility **before** any event to register life-support equipment.
- **Pharmacy emergency refill** — during a declared disaster, many US states allow pharmacists to issue an emergency refill (ranging from a 72-hour to a 30-day supply, depending on state). Ask your pharmacist about their policy and your state's laws **before** an event, not during one.
- **Cold chain** — Styrofoam cooler + frozen water bottles beats a soft cooler. Insulation ratio matters; keep meds wrapped and off direct ice. A cheap fridge thermometer tells you if the chain held.
- **Go-bag med pouch** — 7–14 days of every daily med, printed Rx list, prescriber contacts, insurance card, medical alert info, pharmacy phone, and a hard copy of this card.
