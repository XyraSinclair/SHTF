# Wildfire Evacuation

Wildfire is one of the few scenarios where **early go** is almost always correct. Staying is a very bad default.

## If you only read one line

**Leave early. The people who die in wildfires are disproportionately those who tried to defend their home, waited for mandatory evacuation, or evacuated late and got caught on the road.** If there is any evacuation advisory — go.

## Detection

- **Watch Duty** (`watchduty.org`, free app) is the best real-time wildfire tracking in the US West. Install it before fire season.
- **Local fire authority Twitter / emergency alerts** (county-level).
- **Visible smoke, unusual heat, falling ash.** If you can see smoke, act.
- **AQI spike to 300+** in clear weather suggests nearby fire.
- **ALERT WILDFIRE cameras** (`alertwildfire.org`) provide live feeds from mountaintop cameras across CA/OR/WA/NV/ID.

## The three-level alert system (common in western US)

- **Level 1: Be Ready.** Fire in the area; begin preparation. Pack the go-bag in the car. Fuel the tank. Move livestock.
- **Level 2: Be Set.** Leave if you are vulnerable (elderly, mobility-limited, with animals, with kids). Everyone else should be ready to leave in minutes.
- **Level 3: Go Now.** Immediate evacuation. Do not stop for anything not already packed.

**Go at Level 1 if you have any doubt.** Roads close at Level 3. Fire moves faster than traffic.

## First 15 minutes (Level 3 issued, or fire visibly approaching)

1. **Do not stop for "just one more thing."**
2. Assemble household and pets at the car.
3. Load: go-bags, documents packet, meds, laptops, phones and chargers, any irreplaceable item that fits.
4. Dress for fire: **long pants, long sleeves, natural fibers** (cotton, wool — not synthetics, which melt). Sturdy shoes. Bandana or N95 around neck.
5. Close (but do not lock) windows and doors. Move flammable furniture away from windows if 60+ seconds to spare.
6. Leave outside lights on (helps firefighters locate the house in smoke).
7. Turn off gas at the meter if you know how.
8. Drive to your pre-planned evacuation destination.

## Driving through smoke / near fire

- **Headlights on.** Visibility can drop to zero in minutes.
- **Windows closed, vents on recirculate.** Smoke and embers in the vehicle are dangerous.
- **Drive slowly.** Pedestrians, abandoned vehicles, and emergency vehicles use the same roads.
- **If trapped by fire in the vehicle**: close windows/vents, cover body with wool blanket or coat (cotton works), lie on the floor, stay in the vehicle. Wait for fire to pass. Cars survive fire fronts surprisingly often if you stay inside.
- **Do not try to outrun through thick smoke.** Zero visibility + panic = head-on collision. Pull over, low, wait.

## If trapped on foot

- Find the largest paved or mineral-soil clearing you can.
- Lie face down, feet toward fire. Cover with non-synthetic clothing if available.
- Shield airway with a cloth, ideally damp. Breathe through the cloth, close to the ground.
- Water, pond, creek, or even a deep ditch offers significant survival advantage.
- Fire fronts pass in minutes; survive the front and you have a chance.

## If you decide to stay and defend (not recommended for most households)

Only consider if:

- Your home has Class-A roofing (metal, tile — not shake or composite).
- Defensible space is maintained (see "Before it happens").
- You have independent water source + pump + hoses not dependent on grid power.
- You have training in structure defense.
- Multiple able adults.
- Nobody vulnerable in the house.

Even then: understand that local authorities may mandate evacuation, and emergency services may not reach you during the fire. Over 80% of structure-loss casualties in recent California fires were inside "defending" homes.

If you defend:

- Clothing: cotton, long, covered. Respirator or N95. Eye protection.
- Close all windows and doors; seal vents with damp cloth.
- Keep all lights on.
- Fill tubs, sinks, buckets with water.
- Move flammable cushions indoors.
- Wet down the immediate perimeter repeatedly as fire approaches.
- Go inside before the front arrives. Stay inside until it passes (60–90 seconds of peak heat).
- Go back outside after the front passes to extinguish embers and spot fires. Embers cause most structure losses.

## Smoke exposure (indoor sheltering from distant fire)

If the fire is not near enough to evacuate but smoke is heavy:

- **Close windows and doors.** Seal large gaps with damp towels or tape.
- **HVAC on recirculate** with MERV-13+ filter.
- **Run a HEPA purifier** (or build a Corsi-Rosenthal box — 20" box fan + four MERV-13 filters + cardboard, ~$80 in parts). This is one of the single most effective DIY preparations for wildfire season.
- **Reduce indoor sources of combustion**: no candles, no cooking with open flame if possible, no smoking.
- **N95 respirators** for any outdoor movement. Cloth masks do nothing against smoke particulates.

AQI thresholds:
- 0–100: normal activity.
- 100–150: sensitive groups limit outdoors.
- 150–300: everyone limits outdoors; use respirators if outside.
- 300+: stay indoors, run filters continuously.
- 500+: extended exposure is clearly harmful; evacuation becomes a reasonable decision.

## First 24 hours after evacuation

- Confirm arrival at destination with out-of-area contact.
- Register with the Red Cross or local emergency management — they track displaced persons for family reunification.
- **Do not return** until authorities give all-clear. Roads are unstable, power lines down, and embers can reignite structures days later.
- Your property inventory video is now worth thousands in the insurance claim (see [`../tier-1-setup/04-cash-and-documents.md`](../tier-1-setup/04-cash-and-documents.md)).

## Returning home after a fire

- Enter only after official all-clear.
- **Hazmat concerns**: ash contains heavy metals, asbestos from older buildings, and combustion products. Wear:
  - N95 or P100 respirator
  - Goggles
  - Gloves
  - Long sleeves/pants
  - Sturdy boots
- Avoid hot spots; they can reignite.
- Do not drink tap water until cleared — pipes and storage may have melted and off-gassed contaminants. Even cold taps can carry benzene after severe wildfires.
- Document damage photographically before cleaning anything.
- Call insurance company from the site.

## Longer-term smoke / drought / fire season

For sustained fire season (weeks to months of elevated risk):

- Keep vehicles fueled.
- Keep go-bags in vehicles.
- Keep Watch Duty running in the background.
- Check in on elderly and immunocompromised neighbors — smoke hits them hardest.
- Accept that you may evacuate multiple times in a season. False alarms are cheaper than the alternative.

## Before it happens, do this once

### Defensible space (for property owners)

- **Zone 1** (0–5 ft from structure): non-flammable ground cover, no mulch, no flammable plants, metal-framed screens on all vents. Ember-resistant zone.
- **Zone 2** (5–30 ft): well-watered low vegetation, no dead wood, trees limbed up 10 ft, separation between tree crowns.
- **Zone 3** (30–100 ft): thinned trees, cleared undergrowth, minimal flammable fuels.

Citation: CAL FIRE Ready for Wildfire program; state forestry equivalents (DNR/ODF) publish the same.

### House hardening

- Class-A roofing (metal, tile, asphalt shingle — not shake).
- Ember-resistant vent screens (1/8" mesh, or smaller).
- Enclosed eaves.
- Double-pane tempered windows.
- Non-combustible siding (fiber cement, stucco, brick, metal).
- Gutters cleaned regularly — leaf-filled gutters ignite from embers and take the roof with them.

### Logistics

1. Install Watch Duty. Turn on notifications for your county.
2. Identify your evacuation zone (county GIS — search "[your county] evacuation zones").
3. Plan **two evacuation routes**. Main fires cut off main roads.
4. Confirm primary and backup evacuation destinations. See [`../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md`](../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md).
5. Make a "grab list" taped inside a closet door — what loads into the car, in what order, in 5 minutes. Print. Review annually.
6. Take photos and video of every room, every closet, every valuable item. Store encrypted off-site.
7. Pre-staged pet carriers, especially if animals are fearful (cats hide when you need them most). Practice loading them on a calm afternoon.
