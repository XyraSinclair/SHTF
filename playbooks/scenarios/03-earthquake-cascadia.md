# Earthquake (Cascadia + general)

If you live in the Pacific Northwest, earthquakes are the scenario you are most statistically likely to face at disaster scale. The Cascadia Subduction Zone megaquake is not speculative — it is overdue. USGS estimates a **~15% probability in the next 50 years** for a full-margin M9.0 rupture, and significantly higher for a partial rupture.

This playbook is general-purpose (works anywhere) but calls out Cascadia specifics.

## If you only read one line

**Drop, Cover, Hold On** during shaking. **Do not run outside.** Most injuries in earthquakes come from falling objects and from running through doorways and broken glass mid-shaking.

## Detection

- **ShakeAlert** (iOS and Android in CA/OR/WA) sends warning seconds before shaking reaches you. Enable it. Seconds matter.
- Some older iPhones show **Earthquake Warnings** natively.
- **USGS earthquake feed** for post-event confirmation: `earthquake.usgs.gov`.

## During shaking (the first 90 seconds)

### If you are indoors

1. **Drop** to hands and knees before the shaking knocks you down.
2. **Cover** your head and neck with your arms. Crawl under a sturdy desk or table if close. If no cover, move against an interior wall, away from windows.
3. **Hold On** to whatever you're under. Shaking can last 60+ seconds in a major event; the Cascadia megaquake may shake for 3–5 minutes.

**Myth**: the "triangle of life" (curl next to furniture) is actively dangerous and contradicted by every earthquake engineering agency. Follow Drop/Cover/Hold.

**Do not run outside.** Most injuries occur from people moving during shaking. Falling glass, façades, and interior fixtures cause the majority of injuries.

**Avoid**: windows, tall furniture, chandeliers, mirrors, fireplaces, heavy appliances.

### If you are in bed

- Stay in bed. Cover your head with a pillow.
- Unless a heavy light fixture or piece of furniture is directly overhead — then roll to the floor next to the bed.

### If you are outdoors

- Move away from buildings, power lines, trees, overhead wires.
- Drop to the ground in an open area. Stay there until shaking stops.
- Be aware of falling façades, glass, and chimneys even a few seconds after shaking stops.

### If you are driving

- Pull over to a clear area, away from bridges, overpasses, power lines, trees, and signs.
- Stop. Set the parking brake. Stay inside until shaking stops.
- Do not try to drive over bridges or overpasses — they may be compromised.

### If you are in a high-rise

- Drop, Cover, Hold.
- **Do not use elevators.** Power and elevator systems may fail.
- Expect sprinklers and fire alarms to trigger — they are sensitive.
- Stay inside the building during shaking.

### If you are on the coast (Cascadia)

**Cascadia tsunami warning.** If you feel strong shaking at the coast or coastal inlets, a tsunami is likely within 15–30 minutes.

- **Do not wait for official warning.** The first wave may arrive before sirens.
- **As soon as shaking stops: go inland and up.** Minimum 100 ft elevation OR 2 miles inland. Do not drive if you can walk — roads will jam.
- Vertical evacuation buildings (engineered tall structures) are marked in some coastal towns. Know yours.
- Tsunami waves come in series. The first is not always the largest. Do not return for hours after official all-clear.

Citation: Washington State Emergency Management tsunami planning maps; `survival-guides/FM21-76_US_Army_Survival_Manual.pdf`.

## Immediately after shaking stops

### First 5 minutes

1. **Check yourself**, then the people near you.
2. **Look for fire.** Gas leaks + broken electrical = fire risk.
3. **Smell for gas.** If you smell gas, shut off the main gas valve outside (see below), leave the building, call the gas company from outside.
4. **Put on sturdy shoes.** Broken glass is everywhere.
5. **Expect aftershocks.** They will be frequent for hours to days.

### First 30 minutes

- **Shut off utilities if damaged.**
  - Gas: only if you smell it or hear hissing. Once off, only a professional should turn it back on. Where is your gas shutoff? (It should be on your household roster — see [`../tier-1-setup/01-household-roster.md`](../tier-1-setup/01-household-roster.md).)
  - Water: shut off at the main if you have broken pipes.
  - Electricity: shut off at the breaker if you see damage to the panel or wiring.
- **Check structural integrity.** Cracks in chimneys, foundations, walls? Leaning porches? Any "yes" = don't enter or re-enter.
- **Communicate.** Text your out-of-area contact. See [`../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md`](../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md). Keep voice calls short. Long-distance routes when local doesn't.
- **Listen to NOAA or AM radio.** Battery-powered or hand-crank. Rumors will be rampant; verify before acting.

### First 6 hours

- Move injured people to a triage area outside if structure is unsafe. See [`../frameworks/triage.md`](../frameworks/triage.md).
- **Check on neighbors**, especially elderly, disabled, and anyone living alone. Neighbor-checks save more lives than first responders in the first hours of a mass-casualty earthquake.
- Inventory: water, food, first aid, flashlights, radios, medications.
- Reassess shelter: is your home habitable tonight? If not, go to your primary evacuation destination or a designated shelter.
- Document damage with photos before moving anything (insurance).

## First 24 hours

### Water

After a major quake, assume tap water is contaminated. Treat all water — boiling 1 minute at sea level (3 min above 6,500 ft), or bleach 2 drops per liter + 30 min wait. Water heater tanks are a 30–50-gallon potable reserve — see [`../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md`](../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md).

### Food

- Fridge: eat the perishables first. 4-hour rule.
- Freezer: unopened, full = 48h.
- Canned goods: check for dents, rust, bulging. Discard questionable cans.

### Sanitation

Toilets may not flush. A single flush requires 1–2 gallons. Alternatives:

- Bucket toilet with sawdust or kitty litter (or the "twin-bucket" system).
- See `survival-guides/sanitation/Emergency_Toilet_Guidebook.pdf` for specifics.

### Aftershocks

Stronger earthquakes are often followed by aftershocks of magnitude 1–2 smaller, sometimes within the first few hours. Prepare for structure damage to worsen.

## First week

### If your home is habitable

- Continue shelter in place.
- Ration water and food — Cascadia scenarios predict **weeks** of utility disruption, not days.
- Keep the bathtub filled; collect rainwater if possible.
- Pool resources with neighbors. Isolated households fare worse.
- Cook outside (grills, camp stoves). Never indoors or in garages.
- Sanitation discipline prevents dysentery outbreaks, which are the actual post-earthquake mass killers. See `survival-guides/FM21-10_Field_Hygiene_Sanitation.pdf`.

### If your home is not habitable

- Move to your evacuation destination. If roads are passable, drive. If not, prepare to stay at a designated shelter until transport opens.
- Keep a visible note on your door: "We are safe, at [destination]. [Date, time, name]."

### Infrastructure timeline (Cascadia-scale, per Washington State)

| Service | Likely outage in major Cascadia event |
|---------|--------------------------------------|
| Electricity | 1–3 months in coastal and rural areas |
| Water/sewer | 1–3 months |
| Natural gas | Weeks to months |
| Cell service | Days to weeks |
| Highways | Weeks for main routes; months for coastal roads 101 |
| Bridges | Several major bridges may be unusable; bypass routes limited |
| Ports/fuel | Months to recover |
| Medical care | Regional hospitals overwhelmed for weeks |

This is why 14-day prep is the floor, not the ceiling, in Cascadia.

## Stabilization (week 2+)

- FEMA Disaster Recovery Centers open after federal declaration. Register via `disasterassistance.gov` or phone when you can.
- Document all damage and losses for insurance and federal claims.
- Watch for scams: door-to-door contractors, fake FEMA reps, fraudulent donation requests.
- Mental health: delayed PTSD is common after major earthquakes. Do not normalize being "fine" at month 2 if you are not.

## Specific prep for Cascadia / PNW

- **Two Weeks Ready** (Washington State EM campaign) recommends 14 days of water, food, meds, and cash.
- **Secure heavy furniture** to wall studs. Bookshelves, dressers, water heaters, televisions.
- **Cabinet latches** on kitchen cabinets, especially above sink.
- **Strap water heater** to wall studs. Both top and bottom.
- **Gas shutoff wrench** attached to the meter — a crescent wrench works. Know how to use it.
- **Know your tsunami zone.** Map: `maps/topo/` has USGS topos; for tsunami zones specifically, search "DOGAMI tsunami [Oregon]" or "Washington DNR tsunami maps" while you still have internet, and save offline.
- **Know your liquefaction zone.** Houses built on fill land fare worst. Check Washington DNR or Oregon DOGAMI maps.

## Kids and earthquakes

- Practice Drop/Cover/Hold at home. It is the school drill; it should also be the home drill.
- Teach them what to do if separated (go to the meeting point — see [`../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md`](../tier-1-setup/05-family-comms-plan.md)).
- Keep a pair of shoes under every kid's bed. A child walking on broken glass barefoot is one of the most common injuries.

## Before it happens, do this once

1. Enable ShakeAlert (CA/OR/WA) on every phone in the household.
2. Walk through every room: what falls, what shatters, what pins? Secure it this weekend.
3. Put shoes and a flashlight under every bed.
4. Strap the water heater.
5. Locate and label gas, water, electrical shutoffs. Add to household roster.
6. If you live on the coast: drive your tsunami evacuation route in daylight and at night.
7. Build the 14-day kit per [`../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md`](../tier-1-setup/03-water-food-stockpile.md).
